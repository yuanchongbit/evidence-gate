The Evidence Gate

Hints dilute and rotate the evidence axis
of vision-language models

yuanchongbit

Code Paper Results Limits BibTeX

TL;DR. Ask a vision-language model about an object that is not in the image and it usually says unknown. Add one sentence — “Hint: the umbrella is black.” — and it answers black. We show that this is not a failure of seeing. The evidence is still decodable inside the model, but the hint shortens its mean difference and turns it 24°–71° off the direction the model was reading (40° in the 3B model below); what breaks is the gate that turns evidence into abstention. The gate is written late, in the last few layers, and can be read out and pushed back along a single direction. The geometry repeats in four backbones from two language-model families; only the collapse of the model’s own confidence is specific to the Qwen readouts.

One image, six prompts, no editing

Every photograph appears in all six cells of a 2×3 within-subject design: visual evidence {object present, object annotation-absent} × textual hint {none, correct, conflicting}.

The absent arm is not produced by erasing pixels. It asks about an object category that COCO’s exhaustive val2017 annotations place outside the image, so the scene is untouched. A separate yes/no probe confirms the manipulation: p(yes) = 0.095 for absent categories versus 0.95 for the original images. Absence is annotation-level, not ground-truth visual absence.

The hint is made plausible rather than adversarial: it names the absent category’s most frequent colour in COCO val. Correct hints help (accuracy 0.762 → 0.973), which is what makes the conflicting hint a causal manipulation rather than noise.

The output readout collapses in the Qwen family. The evidence axis dilutes and rotates in all four.

Under a conflicting hint, every signal computed from the output distribution of the three Qwen-backed models (the two Qwen2.5-VL sizes and LLaVA-OneVision, whose language model is Qwen2-7B) loses the ability to tell “evidence present” from “evidence absent”. A linear probe on the final hidden state keeps most of its ranking on held-out scenes (0.958 in 3B, 0.946 in 7B, 0.935 in LLaVA), but its present–absent gap halves (15.9 → 7.3) and the full mean difference both shrinks and turns. The information is re-encoded, not erased. The fourth backbone, Gemma-3-4B-it (Gemma-3 with a SigLIP encoder, no Qwen component), shows the same geometry but not the output collapse: its p(unknown) AUROC falls only from 0.961 to 0.891, its maximum probability rises from 0.627 to 0.701, and its held-out probe transfers at 0.964 / 0.970.

The suppression is late; the evidence is diluted and rotated

Under conflict the probe’s present–absent gap shrinks from 15.9 to 7.3, while the within-class spread is unchanged. Measured in the full state space (table below), the present-minus-absent mean difference keeps 0.39–0.64 of its length and turns 24°–71° away from the no-hint axis; the two hinted colours rotate to nearly the same direction (cos 0.91 in the Qwen models, 0.97 in Gemma). Whitened by within-class noise the separation does not fall, and a probe refit on the conflict pair reaches 0.989–0.999: the evidence is re-coded rather than destroyed. This is why p(unknown) fails — it reads an absolute scale and a fixed direction — while rank-based gating survives.

The same estimator applied to the no-hint vector against itself returns ratio 1.00 and an angle of 3–5°, the noise floor. Per-scene gaps lose cross-scene coherence while noise-normalised separation does not fall: the aggregate shortens partly because different scenes stop pointing the same way. Gemma-3-4B-it (Gemma-3 + SigLIP, no Qwen component) reproduces the dilution, the rotation, the colour-independent direction (cos 0.97) and the held-out transfer (0.964 / 0.970), but not the output collapse: its max-probability rises under conflict (0.627 → 0.701) while its answers are overridden as strongly as in 3B (accuracy 0.567 → 0.241). The geometry generalises across language-model families; the rank–scale dissociation is specific to the Qwen2/2.5 readouts.

Two control interventions close the causal loop. Along the probe direction w, adding +0.5·‖h‖ pushes absent-object refusal from 0.904 to 0.000; subtracting 0.5·‖h‖ raises present-object refusal from 0.011 to 0.352. The dose–response is monotone and survives when the direction is fitted on one half of the scenes and evaluated on the other.

The same direction still works when the hint is present. Applied during the conflicting pass, −0.25·‖h‖ raises absent-object refusal from 0.542 to 0.874, and +0.25·‖h‖ raises true-colour answers from 0.383 to 0.479 while hint adoption stays at 0.433. Conflict dilutes, rotates and under-weights the evidence; it does not disconnect it from the decision. Because the vector is injected immediately before unembedding, this is readout-level causal evidence.

Two controls bound the claim. Ablating w lowers absent refusal by 0.215 / 0.211 / 0.102 (3B / 7B / LLaVA) and raises hint adoption by 0.201 / 0.209 / 0.113, while ablating any of three random directions moves every metric by at most 0.015. That ratio is not direction-specific evidence, because the projections remove different amounts of the state: the mean absolute projection fraction is 0.055 for w versus 0.019 / 0.004 / 0.008 for the random directions, a 5.4× energy difference. Matching the removed energy removes most of the effect: w scaled to a random direction's energy changes absent refusal by −0.073 (3B) and −0.018 (7B), a random direction scaled up to w's energy is null in 3B (+0.010) but larger than w in 7B (−0.044), and class-mean / first-PC directions at the same energy move 3B refusal (+0.083) and leave 7B unchanged (+0.004). Addition is also not specific: large random directions of the same norm can open the gate. The defensible claim is readout-level sufficiency and an efficient readout direction, not an identified circuit.

The effect replicates in the other two models, whose conflict policies are opposite. Qwen2.5-VL-7B over-refuses (absent refusal 0.918); −0.25·‖h‖ raises it to 1.000 and +0.25·‖h‖ lowers it to 0.057. LLaVA-OneVision-7B refuses almost nothing (0.138 absent, 0.004 present); the same sweep gives 0.734 and 0.000. Opening the gate does not choose the answer: it makes LLaVA more hint-adherent (0.833 → 0.990 on absent items) while its present-item true-colour rate stays statistically unchanged (+0.019), and lets 7B recover true answers (0.249 → 0.421) but also adopt the hint more (0.176 → 0.425). The direction is an abstention gate, not an answer selector.

The causal controls replicate. Dose-matched injection is inert through layer 16 (3B) and layer 20 (7B) and only bites at layer 32 / 26; LLaVA's onset is earlier (layer 20) but still in the last 30% of depth. Same-image, opposite-question patching at L24/L26 gives paired absent-minus-present recovery gaps of +0.51 / +0.83 (3B), +0.84 / +0.86 (7B) and +0.74 / +0.80 (LLaVA), while shuffled sources restore at least as much — the late state carries the policy of the question it was recorded under, not a code for this image's evidence.

Turning the mechanism into a gate

Fabrication = answering a colour when the queried object is absent. At a fixed 50% coverage, ranking by the probe instead of by the model’s own confidence removes most fabrications — but only when the prompt is polluted. Pooled over all three hint conditions, the advantage shrinks from 23× to 3× (0.064 vs. 0.193), and a fixed score threshold becomes conservative under conflict because the axis contracts.

The probe advantage is specific to the hidden state: a logistic readout over the same 12 output probabilities fabricates 0.142 under conflict at 50% coverage. The gain survives unanswerable prevalences from 5% to 50%; a 10% conformal risk certificate holds in 3B and LLaVA, and a 5% certificate is infeasible at 130 calibration scenes.

LLaVA is far more hint-sensitive: under conflict its present-object refusal is 0.004 and absent-object refusal 0.138, against 0.556 and 0.918 in Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Yet recovery is localised at the same relative depth, and the axis dilutes and rotates in all three. Sensitivity and locus are separable: a model that is easier to fool has the same gate, pushed further open. Gemma-3-4B was scored on geometry, probe transfer and signal detection but not swept layer by layer, so the depth profile rests on these three.

What we do not claim

Not a universal better confidence

On VizWiz’s naturally unanswerable questions, the model’s own p(unknown) matches the frozen probe (0.878 vs. 0.827). The probe still beats max-probability (0.437), but its advantage over p(unknown) is specific to settings where a hint attacks the refusal gate.

Rank invariance, not calibration invariance

On VQA-v2 colour questions the frozen probe’s mean score moves from +6.93 to +2.10 under conflict even though its ordering is preserved. We claim AUROC invariance, not score invariance.

Evidence, not correctness

The probe answers “is the evidence there?”, not “is this answer right?”. For answer correctness without hints, max-probability is the better signal.

Absence is not scene fit

Absent categories are sampled without regard to whether they fit the scene, and a COCO co-occurrence score predicts no-hint refusal (better-fitting half refuses less by 0.116 / 0.069 / 0.132 across 3B / 7B / LLaVA). Category frequency is only weakly related to it (Spearman 0.23) and does not explain it: jointly fitted, scene fit stays negative in all three models while frequency adds only in 7B. Evidence detection survives in both halves — p(unknown) AUROC at least 0.976 and probe at least 0.989 — so scene fit moves the level of the gate, not the ranking of evidence.

Colour is the deep test

Side and count tasks replicate the gate opening but not the full readout collapse; they are boundary conditions. Mechanistic claims are strongest on colour.

Readout-level causal evidence

Steering is injected immediately before unembedding, and the patching control’s shuffled source is label-matched. We claim sufficiency at the readout and localisation of the recovery, not the full upstream mechanism.

The output collapse is model-specific

Gemma-3-4B dilutes and rotates its evidence axis as strongly as the Qwen models, yet its own confidence barely degrades (p(unknown) AUROC 0.961 → 0.891, maximum probability 0.627 → 0.701) while its answers are overridden just the same. The collapse that motivates a frozen gate is a property of the Qwen2/2.5 readouts; in Gemma the probe’s margin over p(unknown) is smaller (0.964 vs. 0.891) but still present. SmolVLM-Instruct, a further backbone, never abstains at all, so it cannot test the gate.
