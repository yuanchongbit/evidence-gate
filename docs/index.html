The Evidence Gate

Hints dilute and rotate the evidence axis
of vision-language models

Chong Yuan

Code Paper Results Limits BibTeX

TL;DR. Ask a vision-language model about an object that is not in the image and it usually says unknown. Add one sentence — “Hint: the umbrella is black.” — and it answers black. We show that this is not a failure of seeing. The evidence is still decodable inside the model, but the hint shortens its mean difference and turns it 24°–86° off the no-hint linear-readout direction (40° in the 3B model below); what breaks is the gate that turns evidence into abstention. The gate is written late, in the last third of the network (69–94% depth), and can be read out and pushed back along a single direction. The geometry repeats in five backbones from three language-model families; the collapse of the model’s own confidence appears in the Qwen and Phi readouts but not in Gemma, so it is readout-specific rather than family-wide.

One image, six prompts, no editing

Every photograph appears in all six cells of a 2×3 within-subject design: visual evidence {object present, object annotation-absent} × textual hint {none, hint a, hint b}. On the present arm hint a names the object’s true colour and hint b a deliberately far-hue distractor; on the absent arm no colour can be true, so a names a colour the absent category often has in COCO val and b a far-hue one.

The absent arm is not produced by erasing pixels. It asks about an object category that COCO’s exhaustive val2017 annotations place outside the image, so the scene is untouched. A separate yes/no probe confirms the manipulation: p(yes) = 0.095 for absent categories versus 0.95 for the original images. Absence is annotation-level, not ground-truth visual absence.

Aligned hints help (accuracy 0.762 → 0.973), which is what makes the conflicting ones a causal manipulation rather than noise.

The output readout collapses in Qwen and Phi. The evidence axis dilutes and rotates in all five.

Under a conflicting hint, every signal computed from the output distribution of the three Qwen-backed models (the two Qwen2.5-VL sizes and LLaVA-OneVision, whose language model is Qwen2-7B) loses the ability to tell “evidence present” from “evidence absent”. A linear probe on the final hidden state keeps most of its ranking on held-out scenes (0.958 in 3B, 0.946 in 7B, 0.935 in LLaVA), but its present–absent gap halves (15.9 → 7.3) and the full mean difference both shrinks and turns. The information is re-encoded, not erased. Gemma-3-4B-it (Gemma-3 with a SigLIP encoder, no Qwen component) shows the same geometry but not the output collapse: its p(unknown) AUROC falls only from 0.961 to 0.891, its maximum probability rises from 0.627 to 0.701, and its held-out probe transfers at 0.964 / 0.970. Phi-3.5-vision (Phi-3 with a CLIP encoder) is the opposite case: it abstains (0.897) and is overridden (0.322 / 0.425), rotates its evidence vector 86° / 74° so that a no-hint probe falls to 0.685 / 0.675, yet a refit still decodes the same states at 0.998, and its own signal collapses to below chance (0.939 → 0.385 / 0.458).

The suppression is late; the evidence is diluted and rotated

Under conflict the probe’s present–absent gap shrinks from 15.9 to 7.3, while the within-class spread shrinks much less (0.62–0.83× across models, against a mean gap of 0.20–0.69×). Measured in the full state space (table below), the present-minus-absent mean difference keeps 0.39–0.64 of its length and turns 24°–71° away from the no-hint axis in the four fitted models; the two hinted colours rotate to nearly the same direction (cos 0.91 in the Qwen models, 0.97 in Gemma). Whitened by within-class noise the separation does not fall, and a probe refit on the conflict pair reaches 0.989–0.999: the evidence is re-coded rather than destroyed. This is why p(unknown) fails — it reads an absolute scale and a fixed direction — while rank-based gating survives. Quantitatively, across 12 model–condition cells, d′c/d′0 = 1.42 × (gap ratio) + 0.003 (R² = 0.918), with a per-model gain between 1.25 and 1.60, because the within-class spread contraction is nearly condition-independent (0.71 ± 0.07). The gain is not shared across models, and this regularity stops at the readout: it does not predict the models’ own confidence collapse. Replacing the probe with a behavioural d′ computed from outputs alone removes the identity: over the eight hinted cells the behavioural ratio follows the same gap ratio (slope 0.97 with intercept 0.12, R² = 0.57; through-origin 1.27), with a model-specific gain of 0.92 (3B), 1.43 (7B), 1.62 (LLaVA) and 1.40 (Gemma). Out of sample, the probe law holds when the per-model gain is estimated inside the model (leave-one-cell-out error 0.056 against 0.179 for a unit slope; scene-bootstrap slope 1.42 [1.37, 1.47]) but the gain does not extrapolate to an unseen model (leave-one-model-out 0.02–0.12); the behavioural fit is descriptive rather than predictive (leave-one-cell-out 0.100 against 0.121–0.125 baselines).

The same estimator applied to the no-hint vector against itself returns ratio 1.00 and an angle of 3–5°, the noise floor. Per-scene gaps lose cross-scene coherence while noise-normalised separation does not fall: the aggregate shortens partly because different scenes stop pointing the same way. Gemma-3-4B-it reproduces the dilution, the rotation, the colour-independent direction (cos 0.97) and the held-out transfer (0.964 / 0.970), but not the output collapse: its max-probability rises under conflict (0.627 → 0.701) while its answers are overridden as strongly as in 3B (accuracy 0.567 → 0.241). Phi-3.5-vision pushes the geometry furthest: at 86° / 74° the no-hint probe falls to 0.685 / 0.675 although a refit reaches 0.998, and its own signal collapses to 0.385 / 0.458. The geometry therefore generalises across three language-model families and three vision encoders; the output collapse appears in the Qwen and Phi readouts but not in Gemma, so it is readout-specific rather than family-wide. A second stimulus set built on PASCAL VOC 2012 (180 images, automatic labels, same 3B checkpoint) reproduces the behaviour, the frozen transfer (0.925 / 0.950), the geometry (0.41 / 0.52, 35° / 33°) and the gate (0.367 → 0.144 at 25% coverage).

Two control interventions close the causal loop. Along the probe direction w, adding +0.5·‖h‖ pushes absent-object refusal from 0.904 to 0.000; subtracting 0.5·‖h‖ raises present-object refusal from 0.011 to 0.352. The dose–response is monotone and survives when the direction is fitted on one half of the scenes and evaluated on the other.

The same direction still works when the hint is present. Applied during the conflicting pass, −0.25·‖h‖ raises absent-object refusal from 0.542 to 0.874, and +0.25·‖h‖ raises true-colour answers from 0.383 to 0.479 while hint adoption stays at 0.433. Conflict dilutes, rotates and under-weights the evidence; it does not disconnect it from the decision. Because the vector is injected immediately before unembedding, this is readout-level causal evidence.

Two controls bound the claim. Ablating w lowers absent refusal by 0.215 / 0.211 / 0.102 (3B / 7B / LLaVA) and raises hint adoption by 0.201 / 0.209 / 0.113, while ablating any of three random directions moves every metric by at most 0.015. That ratio is not direction-specific evidence, because the projections remove different amounts of the state: the mean absolute projection fraction is 0.055 for w versus 0.019 / 0.004 / 0.008 for the random directions, a 5.4× energy difference. Matching the removed energy removes most of the effect: w scaled to a random direction's energy changes absent refusal by −0.073 (3B) and −0.018 (7B), a random direction scaled up to w's energy is null in 3B (+0.010) but larger than w in 7B (−0.044), and class-mean / first-PC directions at the same energy move 3B refusal (+0.083) and leave 7B unchanged (+0.004). Addition is also not specific: large random directions of the same norm can open the gate. The defensible claim is readout-level sufficiency, not an identified circuit; the direction is efficient in the Qwen-family models and Phi, but not in Gemma, where any same-norm perturbation moves the gate. Energy-matched removal in the two non-Qwen models separates injection from necessity. In Gemma the raw ablation is null (0.437 against a base of 0.439) and stays null under both matched variants, while class-mean and first-PC directions at w's energy raise refusal by +0.021 each: the head readout depends on class structure, not on w. In Phi the raw effect (−0.057) is mostly removed energy; matched down to a random direction's energy it shrinks to −0.013, a random direction scaled up to w's energy moves refusal more and in the opposite direction (+0.048), and class-mean / first-PC directions at matched energy lower it by 0.157 / 0.249. Injection efficiency and removal relevance dissociate.

The effect replicates in the other two models, whose conflict policies are opposite. Qwen2.5-VL-7B over-refuses (absent refusal 0.918); −0.25·‖h‖ raises it to 1.000 and +0.25·‖h‖ lowers it to 0.057. LLaVA-OneVision-7B refuses almost nothing (0.138 absent, 0.004 present); the same sweep gives 0.734 and 0.000. Opening the gate does not choose the answer: it makes LLaVA more hint-adherent (0.833 → 0.990 on absent items) while its present-item true-colour rate stays statistically unchanged (+0.019), and lets 7B recover true answers (0.249 → 0.421) but also adopt the hint more (0.176 → 0.425). The direction is an abstention gate, not an answer selector.

The causal controls replicate. Dose-matched injection is inert through layer 16 (3B) and layer 20 (7B) and only bites at layer 32 / 26; LLaVA's onset is earlier (layer 20) but still in the last 30% of depth. Same-image, opposite-question patching at L24/L26 gives paired absent-minus-present recovery gaps of +0.51 / +0.83 (3B), +0.84 / +0.86 (7B), +0.74 / +0.80 (LLaVA), +0.71 / +0.77 (Gemma) and +0.71 / +0.77 (Phi), while shuffled sources restore at least as much — the late state carries the policy of the question it was recorded under, not a code for this image's evidence. Readout-level steering also replicates outside the Qwen family with different amplitudes and different direction specificity: at ±0.50·‖h‖ absent refusal moves by +0.056 / −0.109 in Gemma (whose present arm already sits at the 0.008 refusal floor) and +0.435 / −0.330 in Phi, where conflict leaves the present arm refusing 0.548, so the gate is less specific to the absent question. A random-direction control at the head qualifies this: the fitted direction is 3.0× the mean random effect in Phi (12.9× for ablation) but 0.55× in Gemma, where ablating w is null (−0.002) despite removing 5.9× the energy of a random projection. Layer injection is clean only at L30 in Gemma (absent refusal −0.151 / +0.088 at ±0.25, present gold and hint unchanged; L16 and L24 at the negative sign move the gate but wreck the present arm), whereas in Phi the direction already acts at L14 (−0.193 / +0.180) and weakens deeper even though patching recovers only 31% at L14 and 97% at L22. Gemma's head is movable but non-direction-specific; Phi's early injection shows the axis is available before the conflict effect is localised, not where it is computed.

The bracket is not the cause: rotation is generic, compression is query-linked

Five further prompt forms carry the same assertion without a bracketed Hint: prefix: a colleague report, a bare claim, a first-person belief, a system-message assertion and a two-turn dialogue. In Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and Gemma-3-4B the evidence axis shortens to 0.38–0.89 of its no-hint length and rotates 16°–47° in every form, the two hinted colours give nearly parallel conflict axes (cos 0.91–0.999), and a probe fitted on the bracketed form transfers to every other form at AUROC 0.95–0.999. What changes with placement is the readout policy, not the geometry: 3B hint adoption spans 0.042–0.885 across forms, 7B stays at or below 0.05 in all of them, and Gemma reverses the ordering. The length ratio predicts wrong-hint accuracy across forms at r = +0.89 / +0.89 / +0.98 (3B / 7B / Gemma), while its relation to adoption has no consistent sign.

Three sentence controls then separate query content from the extra text. Added to the colleague-report template in both evidence arms, with no hint, a neutral sentence (“A colleague mentioned that the room is quiet.”) moves the 3B evidence axis 42.7° [41.4, 44.2] — as far as the conflicting assertion about the queried category (44.2°) — so rotation is a correlate of rewriting the prompt, not of conflict. Compression is the query-linked signature: the axis shortens monotonically with query relevance in all three models — 0.822 / 0.804 / 0.604 / 0.589 (3B), 0.730 / 0.691 / 0.596 / 0.568 (7B), 1.000 / 0.897 / 0.877 / 0.885 (Gemma) for a neutral sentence, an assertion about another object, an assertion about the queried category with a constant colour, and the conflicting assertion. A colour word about another object is not enough; naming the queried category is. The constant-colour query axis is nearly collinear with the conflict axis (cos 0.976 / 0.988 / 0.997), so changing the asserted colour adds only a small further shortening (0.015 in 3B, 0.028 in 7B, none in Gemma). Behaviour follows in 3B: under evidence, accuracy falls 0.762 → 0.736 → 0.705 → 0.571 → 0.567 across no hint, neutral, other object, queried constant colour and conflict, and the query sentence raises stated-colour adoption from 12.3% to 21.8%. Gemma drops only under the query sentence (0.552 / 0.563 → 0.521) and 7B’s accuracy is flat while adoption rises 7.7% → 14.9%. The conflict effect is therefore compression of the evidence axis, graded by query relevance, with rotation as a non-specific correlate of rewriting the prompt; the length ratio, not the angle, is the quantity that tracks conflict accuracy.

Turning the mechanism into a gate

Fabrication = answering a colour when the queried object is absent. The probe is fitted only on no-hint scenes and scored on held-out scenes, so no evaluation scene contributes a label. At a shared 25% answered coverage, ranking by the probe instead of by the model’s own confidence removes most fabrications when the prompt is polluted. At 50% coverage the gain persists in 3B and LLaVA, but 7B refuses 72% of conflict items and never reaches that operating point.

LLaVA is far more hint-sensitive: under conflict its present-object refusal is 0.004 and absent-object refusal 0.138, against 0.556 and 0.918 in Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Yet recovery is localised at the same relative depth, and the axis dilutes and rotates in all five. Gemma-3-4B and Phi-3.5-vision put the transition later in relative depth: recovery reaches 12% / 90% / 101% at 47% / 71% / 88% in Gemma and 31% / 97% / 102% at 44% / 69% / 88% in Phi, although they have 34 and 32 blocks against 28 in the Qwen-7B and LLaVA. Depth, not layer count, sets the profile. Sensitivity and locus are separable: a model that is easier to fool has the same gate, pushed further open.

What we do not claim

The gate’s margin is partly arm-specific

A category-matched control holds the queried category and the asserted colour fixed within a pair. The output collapse, the in-condition refit and the geometry survive, but the frozen probe’s margin over p(unknown) falls from +0.123 / +0.142 / +0.142 to +0.050 / +0.002 / −0.015 (3B / 7B / LLaVA), and at 25% answered coverage its fabrication advantage shrinks from 0.115 to 0.069 in 3B, disappears in 7B and reverses in LLaVA. Gemma’s margin also reverses within-question (−0.009 against p(unknown)); Phi’s stays positive (+0.145) only because its own signal is below chance, and at the operating point the difference is 0.008. Part of the main-design gain therefore reflects the two arms asking different questions, not the evidence alone.

Not a universal better confidence

On VizWiz’s naturally unanswerable questions, the model’s own p(unknown) matches the frozen probe (0.878 vs. 0.827). The probe still beats max-probability (0.437), but its advantage over p(unknown) is specific to settings where a hint attacks the refusal gate.

Rank invariance, not calibration invariance

On VQA-v2 colour questions the frozen probe’s mean score moves from +6.93 to +2.10 under conflict even though its ordering is preserved. We claim AUROC invariance, not score invariance.

Evidence, not correctness

The probe answers “is the evidence there?”, not “is this answer right?”. For answer correctness without hints, max-probability is the better signal.

Absence is not scene fit

Absent categories are sampled without regard to whether they fit the scene, and a COCO co-occurrence score predicts no-hint refusal (better-fitting half refuses less by 0.116 / 0.069 / 0.132 across 3B / 7B / LLaVA). Category frequency is only weakly related to it (Spearman 0.23) and does not explain it: jointly fitted, scene fit stays negative in all three models while frequency adds only in 7B. Evidence detection survives in both halves — p(unknown) AUROC at least 0.976 and probe at least 0.989 — so scene fit moves the level of the gate, not the ranking of evidence.

Colour is the deep test

Side and count tasks replicate the gate opening but not the full readout collapse; they are boundary conditions. Mechanistic claims are strongest on colour.

Readout-level causal evidence

Steering is injected immediately before unembedding, and the patching control’s shuffled source is label-matched. We claim sufficiency at the readout and localisation of the recovery, not the full upstream mechanism.

The output collapse is model-specific

Gemma-3-4B dilutes and rotates its evidence axis as strongly as the Qwen models, yet its own confidence barely degrades (p(unknown) AUROC 0.961 → 0.891, maximum probability 0.627 → 0.701) while its answers are overridden just the same. Phi-3.5-vision (Phi-3 + CLIP) is the counterexample in the other direction: it dilutes to 0.05 / 0.15 of its length, rotates 86° / 74°, collapses its own signal to 0.385 / 0.458, and its frozen probe falls to 0.685 / 0.675 although a refit reaches 0.998. The collapse that motivates a frozen gate therefore appears in the Qwen2/2.5 and Phi-3 readouts but not in Gemma, so it is readout-specific rather than tied to one language-model family. SmolVLM-Instruct, a further backbone, never abstains at all, so it cannot test the gate.
