The Evidence Gate

How vision-language models decide to answer
without evidence

yuanchongbit

Code Paper Results Limits BibTeX

TL;DR. Ask a vision-language model about an object that is not in the image and it usually says unknown. Add one sentence — “Hint: the umbrella is black.” — and it answers black. We show that this is not a failure of seeing. The evidence is still linearly decodable inside the model; what breaks is the gate that turns evidence into abstention. The gate is written late, in the last few layers, and can be read out and pushed back along a single direction.

One image, six prompts, no editing

Every photograph appears in all six cells of a 2×3 within-subject design: visual evidence {object present, object verified absent} × textual hint {none, correct, conflicting}.

The absent arm is not produced by erasing pixels. It asks about an object category that COCO’s exhaustive val2017 annotations place outside the image, so the scene is untouched. A separate yes/no probe confirms the manipulation: p(yes) = 0.095 for absent categories versus 0.95 for the original images.

The hint is made plausible rather than adversarial: it names the absent category’s most frequent colour in COCO val. Correct hints help (accuracy 0.762 → 0.973), which is what makes the conflicting hint a causal manipulation rather than noise.

The readout collapses. The representation does not.

Under a conflicting hint, every signal computed from the model’s output distribution loses the ability to tell “evidence present” from “evidence absent”. A linear probe on the final hidden state does not move.

The suppression is a late write

We capture the last-token state from an unpolluted no-hint forward pass and paste it into the hinted pass at layer L. If the remaining layers still produce a refusal, the evidence was carried forward rather than recomputed.

Two control interventions close the causal loop. Along the probe direction w, adding +0.5·‖h‖ push absent-object refusal from 0.904 to 0.000; subtracting 0.5·‖h‖ raises present-object refusal from 0.011 to 0.352. The dose–response is monotone and survives when the direction is fitted on one half of the scenes and evaluated on the other.

The same direction still works when the hint is present. Applied during the conflicting pass, −0.25·‖h‖ raises absent-object refusal from 0.542 to 0.874, and +0.25·‖h‖ raises true-colour answers from 0.383 to 0.479 while hint adoption stays at 0.433. Conflict under-weights the evidence; it does not disconnect it from the decision.

Turning the mechanism into a gate

Fabrication = answering a colour when the queried object is absent. At a fixed 50% coverage, ranking by the probe instead of by the model’s own confidence removes most fabrications — but only when the prompt is polluted.

LLaVA is far more hint-sensitive: under conflict its refusal falls from 0.751 to 0.161, while Qwen2.5-VL-7B only moves from 0.958 to 0.877. Yet the suppression is written at the same relative depth. Sensitivity and locus are separable: a model that is easier to fool has the same gate, pushed further open.

What we do not claim

Not a universal better confidence

On VizWiz’s naturally unanswerable questions, the model’s own p(unknown) matches the frozen probe (0.878 vs. 0.827). The probe still beats max-probability (0.437), but its advantage over p(unknown) is specific to settings where a hint attacks the refusal gate.

Rank invariance, not calibration invariance

On VQA-v2 colour questions the frozen probe’s mean score moves from +6.93 to +2.10 under conflict even though its ordering is preserved. We claim AUROC invariance, not score invariance.

Evidence, not correctness

The probe answers “is the evidence there?”, not “is this answer right?”. For answer correctness without hints, max-probability is the better signal.

Colour is the deep test

Side and count tasks replicate the gate opening but not the full readout collapse; they are boundary conditions. Mechanistic claims are strongest on colour.
