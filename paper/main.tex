\title{\bfseries Hints Dilute and Rotate the Evidence Axis\\[2pt]
of Vision-Language Models}

\author{Chong Yuan}

\date{November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Vision-language models often answer when the image lacks the queried evidence, yet retract a correct answer as soon as a prompt sentence contradicts it. Are these failures of seeing or of reporting? We use a $2\times3$ within-subject design---visual evidence $\{$present, annotation-absent$\}$ $\times$ textual hint $\{$none, correct, conflicting$\}$ (the absent arm has no correct colour, so its middle cell asserts a plausible one)---built without editing a pixel: ``absent'' is a category that COCO annotations place outside the photograph, checked by a presence probe. Across 261 images and three VLMs built on Qwen2/2.5 language models, conflict collapses the models' own output signals: $\punk$ falls from AUROC $0.993$ to $0.834$ in Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and maximum probability reaches chance ($0.463$). A probe fitted only on the no-hint pair keeps its ranking on held-out scenes ($0.96$, $0.94$, $0.92$ in 3B, 7B and LLaVA, plausible hint), so the evidence is still there; what changes is its geometry. The full present--absent mean-difference vector shortens to $0.39$--$0.64$ of its no-hint length and turns $24^\circ$--$71^\circ$ away from the no-hint axis, with the two hinted colours rotating to nearly the same direction ($\cos=0.91$); per-scene gaps lose coherence ($0.70\to0.41$ in 7B), while noise-normalised separation does not fall and in-condition refitting recovers at least $0.989$; because the within-class spread contracts by a nearly constant $0.71$, the fixed readout's rank-sensitivity loss is proportional to the mean-gap contraction ($d'$ ratio $=1.42\times$ gap ratio, $R^2=0.92$ across four models, leave-one-cell-out error $0.06$). Two further backbones outside that family repeat the geometry: Gemma-3-4B (Gemma-3 with a SigLIP encoder) at $0.53$ of the length, $24^\circ$--$29^\circ$ of rotation, $\cos=0.97$ and $0.96$ held-out transfer, and Phi-3.5-vision (Phi-3 with a CLIP encoder) at $0.05$--$0.15$ of the length and $74^\circ$--$86^\circ$ of rotation. Gemma's output signal barely degrades while Phi's collapses ($\punk$ $0.939\to0.385$), so the geometry generalises across three language-model families, while the collapse tracks the readout rather than the family. The hint does not erase the evidence; it moves it off the axis the readout was using, and dilutes it. Signal detection separates dilution from criterion shift: sensitivity falls in all three Qwen-family models ($d'$ $3.51\to1.45$, $2.98\to1.24$, $3.44\to1.44$), criterion shifts are inconsistent, most of the flattened curve is the axis. Pasting the no-hint state back at $69$--$94\%$ depth returns $80$--$102\%$ of the lost refusal in all five models, so the gate is written late. Steering shows readout-level sufficiency; energy-matched removal shows no direction-specific necessity, so we claim readout-level sufficiency, not a circuit. A frozen gate cuts conflict fabrication at $25\%$ answered coverage from $0.192$ to $0.077$ (3B), $0.169$ to $0.146$ (7B), $0.138$ to $0.062$ (LLaVA), $0.138$ to $0.015$ (Gemma) and $0.562$ to $0.285$ (Phi), but a category-matched control that holds the question fixed across the label removes the 7B and LLaVA margin, so part of the gain is arm-specific; conformal certification of a fabrication rate is feasible only in a minority of splits at this sample size, and we report the minimum bound instead; on naturally unanswerable VizWiz questions $\punk$ matches the probe. Code is released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ask a vision-language model (VLM) \emph{``What color is the umbrella?''} about a photograph with no umbrella, and it will usually answer \unknown. Prepend one plausible sentence---\emph{``Hint: the umbrella is black.''}---and the same model answers \emph{black} with high confidence. The failure is not that the model cannot see. The object was never there.

Two literatures describe adjacent phenomena. Work on hallucination measures objects a model reports but the image does not contain \citep{rohrbach2018object,li2023pope}. Work on sycophancy measures answers that follow a user's stated belief rather than the input \citep{sharma2023sycophancy,turpin2023language,hong2026tosee}. Both describe behaviour; neither separates two questions that the behaviour confounds: \emph{is the evidence represented?} and \emph{is the decision to abstain driven by that representation?} A model that answers \emph{black} may have lost the evidence, or it may still represent ``no umbrella'' while a later stage overrides abstention.

We separate the two with a design in which the image is held fixed and only the \emph{epistemic status} of the question changes. Each of 261 photographs appears in six cells: the queried object is present or annotated-absent, crossed with no hint, a correct hint, or a conflicting hint. The absent arm requires no image editing: it asks about a category that COCO's instance annotations place outside the image. Every cell shares the same pixels, and the six prompts differ only in what they assert. A separate yes/no probe assigns mean $p(\text{yes})=0.095$ to the absent images versus $0.95$ to the originals; annotation absence is independently checked, but it is not identical to model-perceived absence.

The design yields a dissociation (Figure~\ref{fig:signals}). With no hint, the model's own $\punk$ detects evidence availability almost perfectly (AUROC $0.993$). Under conflict every output-space signal degrades ($\punk$ $0.834$, maximum probability $0.463$, 12-way readout $0.923$), while a probe fitted only on the no-hint pair keeps its ranking on scenes it never saw ($0.995$ refitted, $0.958$ held out, hint \texttt{b}) yet its present--absent geometry changes sharply. Measuring the full mean-difference vector, rather than its projection onto one direction, separates the ways a separation can shrink. Under a conflicting hint the vector shortens to $0.53$ of its no-hint length and turns $40^\circ$ away from the no-hint axis in 3B; the same rotation appears in 7B and LLaVA, in a non-Qwen backbone ($24^\circ$--$29^\circ$ in Gemma-3-4B) and reaches $86^\circ$ in a second one (Phi-3.5-vision), and two different hinted colours rotate to almost the same direction ($\cos=0.91$), so it is systematic rather than colour noise. The along-axis projection, which is what a fixed readout sees, falls further than either factor alone ($0.41$ in 3B), while noise-normalised separation does not fall and a readout refitted inside the condition recovers $0.995$--$0.999$. The hint dilutes and rotates the evidence axis; it does not erase it. Three tests follow. Signal detection shows a sensitivity loss with inconsistent criterion shifts, most of the flattened refusal curve being predicted by the dilution. Patching restores most of the lost abstention only in the final few layers, and a direction injected at the readout controls abstention bidirectionally; we claim sufficiency at the readout, not an identified circuit, because removal at matched energy shows no direction-specific necessity. A frozen gate trained only on the no-hint pair then cuts fabrication under conflict by $2.2$--$2.5\times$ in 3B and LLaVA at a quarter of the items answered, a gain that survives prevalence shift but not the move to naturally unanswerable images; in 7B the probe is nominally ahead of $\punk$ but the difference is within noise.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A controlled construction.} A $2\times3$ within-subject benchmark with no image editing (not fully crossed: absent items have no correct colour, so the middle hint cell is a plausible conflicting one), in which ``evidence absent'' is annotation-absent and independently checked by a presence probe, and in which the conflicting hint is plausibility-matched to the present arm. The 261-item manifest and labels are frozen and released.
\item \textbf{A rank--scale dissociation with a bounded causal claim.} Native output signals collapse under conflict while the internal evidence representation stays rank-decodable on held-out scenes but dilutes and rotates. We measure the full mean-difference geometry rather than a single projection, report scene-held-out transfer rather than refitted performance as the headline, and quantify the link between geometry and rank sensitivity: the fixed readout's $d'$ loss is proportional to the mean-gap contraction, with a per-model gain of $1.25$--$1.60$ ($R^2=0.918$ over 12 model--condition cells). We downgrade the causal claim to readout-level sufficiency after an energy-matched removal audit; and a behavioural check shows the dilution law transfers to the model's own output signal in direction and order of magnitude, with a model-specific gain.
\item \textbf{A deployment result and its boundary.} A scene-cross-fitted linear gate reduces fabrication by $2.2$--$2.5\times$ in 3B and LLaVA, $9.0\times$ in Gemma and $2.0\times$ in Phi at $25\%$ answered coverage, survives prevalence shifts from $5\%$ to $50\%$, and loses its advantage on naturally unanswerable images; in 7B the nominal $1.2\times$ difference has a scene-bootstrap interval of $[-0.062,+0.008]$ and is not distinguishable from zero. A category-matched control, in which both arms ask about the same category, keeps the geometry and the in-condition refit but removes the 7B and LLaVA margin over $\punk$, reverses Gemma's and leaves Phi's at $0.008$, so the gain is partly specific to the main design's change of question; we report both. We test conformal certification explicitly and report that $130$ calibration scenes support a family-wise $10\%$ certificate in only $4/20$ splits, and in no split for the other models, under a consistent risk denominator; the deployment claim is empirical, not distribution-free.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Sycophancy and prompt-induced error.}
Language models shift answers toward a user's stated view \citep{sharma2023sycophancy}, and their explanations can diverge from the evidence that drove the answer \citep{turpin2023language}. Recent work documents the multimodal version: VLMs change visual answers to agree with textual assertions, sometimes while still encoding the original content \citep{hong2026tosee}; the phenomenon is also described as blind faith in text \citep{deng2025words}. These studies are behavioural or input-level. We intervene on hidden states, decompose the decision into sensitivity and criterion, and use the resulting direction to build a gate.

\paragraph{Abstention and selective prediction.}
Selective prediction trades coverage for risk using a confidence score \citep{geifman2017selective}, and has been studied for VQA under distribution shift and on unanswerable questions \citep{whitehead2022reliable}. ReCoVERR reduces over-abstention by acquiring more evidence \citep{srinivasan2024selective}, black-box consistency identifies unreliable answers \citep{khan2024identifying}, and Rep2Act trains a representation-to-action router that turns latent answerability into an explicit abstention action \citep{he2026rep2act}. These methods change the decision procedure or the training objective. We hold both fixed and ask \emph{which representation} the scalar should be computed from, and show that the model's own abstention probability is the wrong representation when a hint is present.

\paragraph{Distribution-free risk control.}
Conformal prediction and conformal risk control give finite-sample guarantees for a chosen loss under exchangeability \citep{angelopoulos2024conformal,bates2021distributionfree}. We apply split conformal risk control with a binomial upper confidence bound to fabrication risk and report that, with $130$ calibration scenes and a consistent answered-set denominator, no family-wise $5\%$ or $10\%$ certificate is feasible; the minimum bound is reported instead. BCEA couples conformal filtering with evidence acquisition \citep{xu2026bcea}, and A$^2$Safe grounds VQA safety decisions in a multi-agent evidence board \citep{xu2026a2safe}; we test whether a fabrication rate can be certified under prompt pollution without new evidence or an agent layer.

\paragraph{Probing and steering internal states.}
Linear probes recover truthfulness, knowledge and uncertainty from hidden states \citep{alain2016understanding,azaria2023internal,burns2023discovering,kadavath2022language,marks2024geometry}. Difference-of-means directions can steer behaviour \citep{turner2023activation,rimsky2024steering,zou2023representation,li2023inference}, and chat-model refusal is mediated by a low-dimensional direction \citep{arditi2024refusal,joad2026more}. Activation steering has been applied to VLM refusal \citep{yang2026steering}. We study a different axis---whether the evidence needed to answer is available---and pair steering with a projection audit that bounds what the ablation can show.

\paragraph{Localisation and hallucination.}
Activation patching replaces a state in one forward pass with a state from another and measures the effect on the output \citep{meng2022locating}. Object hallucination is typically measured by asking about absent objects \citep{rohrbach2018object,li2023pope}. We adopt the logic of absent-object questions but make the comparison within-image and within-question, vary only the textual assertion, and add an internal readout.

\section{Design and Data}

\subsection{The $2\times3$ design}

Each sample is a photograph, a queried category $c$, and a gold colour $g$. The six conditions are:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
 & \multicolumn{3}{c}{textual hint} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-4}
visual evidence & none & correct & conflicting \\
\midrule
object present & \texttt{sufficient|none} & \texttt{sufficient|correct} & \texttt{sufficient|wrong} \\
object absent & \texttt{absent|none} & \texttt{absent|a} & \texttt{absent|b} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In the present arm the correct hint states $g$ and the conflicting hint states a fixed far-hue distractor. In the absent arm there is no correct colour: \texttt{a} is the absent category's most frequent colour in COCO val (plausible for the category), and \texttt{b} applies the same far-hue mapping to that colour. \texttt{b} is therefore plausibility-matched to \texttt{sufficient|wrong}, which also uses a far hue; we use \texttt{b} for the geometry contrast and report \texttt{a} separately throughout. The plausible hint \texttt{a} is the harder deployment case (it induces more credulity) and is the one used for the gate. Every image appears in all six cells, so image identity is held constant; within an arm the queried category is fixed as well. The present and absent arms query different categories by construction, so present-versus-absent contrasts are within-image but not within-question. Appendix~\ref{app:controls} reports a category-matched control that holds the queried category and the asserted colour fixed and varies only the evidence.

\subsection{Construction without image editing}

Starting from COCO val2017 \citep{lin2014coco}, we retain single annotated instances with box short side $\ge 44$ px, area fraction $1.5$--$35\%$, and mask/box fill $\ge 0.45$. Colour is proposed as the area-dominant colour inside the instance mask, with purity and margin gates, then verified by hand on contact sheets. Of 1447 geometric candidates, 413 passed the automatic colour gate and 261 survived manual verification (blue 88, white 79, black 45, green 25, red 15, yellow 9; Appendix~\ref{app:colour}).

For the absent arm we sample a category with no annotation in the image (74 distinct categories appear) and use that category's $\{$first, second$\}$ most frequent colour in val as the hint. The colour is plausible for the category; we do not constrain the category to be plausible for the scene. No pixel is edited; the same JPEG is used in all six cells.

\paragraph{Manipulation check.}
A separate yes/no question (``Is there a $c$ in this image?'') yields mean $p(\text{yes})=0.095$ on 260 category-matched negative images versus $0.95$ on the originals. The absent arm is therefore annotation-absent and independently checked. Because $6.5\%$ of these negatives are judged present with probability above $0.5$, we report a strict-absent sensitivity analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:controls} and treat ``absent'' as annotation-level throughout.

Absence is also not the only difference between the arms: a category that fits the scene is refused less often than one that does not. Scoring each absent category by its COCO train co-occurrence with the objects annotated in the image and splitting the items at the median, no-hint refusal is lower in the better-fitting half by $0.116$ $[0.047,0.185]$ (3B), $0.069$ $[0.023,0.115]$ (7B) and $0.132$ $[0.039,0.224]$ (LLaVA). The evidence axis is unaffected: within each half, $\punk$ still separates present from absent images at AUROC $\ge0.976$ and the internal probe at $\ge0.989$ (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}). Scene fit moves the level of refusal, not the ordering of present against absent. The effect survives a control for category frequency, which is only weakly related to it.

\subsection{Prompts, readout and signals}

The system prompt asks for one word and instructs the model to answer \unknown\ when the image does not contain enough information. The user prompt is
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
[Hint: the \{category\} is \{colour\}.]\newline
What color is the \{category\}?\newline
Answer with one word.
\end{quote}
where the hint line is omitted in the no-hint condition. The readout is a distribution over eleven colour words plus \unknown; all twelve candidates are single tokens in every tokenizer used. Probabilities are the softmax over their logits at the first answer position.

\paragraph{Output signals.} We compute $\punk$, maximum probability over the twelve candidates, the top-two gap, negative entropy, and a 12-dimensional logistic readout over the full candidate distribution. These use the same forward pass as the answer.

\paragraph{Internal probe.} A logistic regression on the final-token hidden state ($2048$-d for 3B, $3584$-d for the two 7B models), with standardised features and $C=0.1$. It is trained on the no-hint pair \texttt{sufficient|none} vs.\ \texttt{absent|none}. Cross-validation is grouped by scene (five folds) so a scene never appears in both train and test; a second analysis groups by queried category to test a category$\to$colour shortcut. The \emph{frozen} protocol fits on the no-hint states of the training scenes and scores the \emph{held-out} scenes' hinted states, so no scene contributes both to fitting and to evaluation; the headline numbers use this protocol at a fixed $C=0.1$. The \emph{refit} protocol retrains inside each condition, still grouped by scene, and is reported only as an upper bound. The direction used for steering uses $C=0.01$ because it is injected at a fixed norm; Appendix~\ref{app:prompts} records both settings and the sensitivity to $C$.

\paragraph{Steering and patching.} The direction $\wdir$ is the logistic-regression probe direction fitted on the no-hint pair. We inject $\alpha\lVert h\rVert\wdir$ at the final position, where $\lVert h\rVert$ is the median hidden-state norm and $\alpha\in\{\pm0.25,\pm0.5\}$, and ablate by projecting $\wdir$ out of the state. A split-half check fits $\wdir$ on 130 scenes and evaluates on the remaining 131. For patching, layer $L$ receives the final-token state from a no-hint pass and the remaining layers run; a shuffled-source control pastes another sample's no-hint state at the same layer.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Evidence detection is AUROC over the 522 paired items, with scene-grouped bootstrap confidence intervals (2000 resamples) and paired bootstrap intervals for differences. Selective answering is reported as the fabrication rate among answered items at matched answered coverage; $25\%$ is the shared operating point (feasible for every model), and $50\%$ is reported where the model's own answering rate allows it. We report unadjusted $95\%$ intervals and treat them as descriptive: the primary contrasts exclude zero by wide margins, while the own-axis residual reported below is marginal and is labelled as such.

\paragraph{Models.} Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 7B \citep{bai2025qwen25vl} in bfloat16 with SDPA attention and at most $1024^2$ pixels, and LLaVA-OneVision-7B \citep{li2024llavaov} with the longest image side at most $512$ px, loaded through \texttt{AutoModelForImageTextToText}. All LLaVA stages use the same $512$ px preprocessing. All experiments ran on a single 24 GB A10.

Table~\ref{tab:behaviour} shows the basic pattern. Without hints the models are conservative in the right way: 3B refuses $90.4\%$ of absent-object questions and $1.1\%$ of present-object ones. A conflicting hint moves both arms toward the assertion: refusal on the absent arm falls to $52.1\%$ (hint a) and $56.3\%$ (hint b), and $43$--$47\%$ of answers name the hinted colour. On the present arm the same hint raises refusal from $1.1\%$ to $8.8\%$ and drops accuracy from $76.2\%$ to $38.3\%$. A correct hint does the opposite, raising accuracy to $97.3\%$. The hint is therefore treated as evidence, not noise: it helps when true and hurts when false. The two absent hints differ in effect size but not in sign, and the far-hue hint \texttt{b} is the closer match to the present-arm manipulation.

The base policies differ sharply. Qwen2.5-VL-7B over-refuses under conflict ($55.6\%$ on present items) and adopts the hint on only $17.6\%$; LLaVA-OneVision is close to fully credulous (present refusal $0.4\%$, absent-hint adoption $76$--$90\%$). Gemma-3-4B-it---Gemma-3 with a SigLIP encoder, one of two backbones here whose language model is not from the Qwen family (the other is Phi-3.5-vision, Appendix~\ref{app:phi})---sits between them: it refuses $84.3\%$ of absent items without a hint, conflict lowers this to $40.2\%$ (hint a) and $47.5\%$ (hint b) with adoption of $57.9\%$ and $50.2\%$, and on present items the same hint is adopted on $67.4\%$ and drops accuracy from $56.7\%$ to $24.1\%$. Its colour reading without a hint is weaker than 3B's, so the present arm is noisier, but the direction of both arms is the same. All five show the dilution and rotation documented next (Phi-3.5 in Appendix~\ref{app:phi}), which is why we treat the geometry as the invariant and the behavioural form as model-specific.

Table~\ref{tab:signals} and Figure~\ref{fig:signals} contain the central result. With no hint, $\punk$ is already an excellent evidence detector ($0.993$), statistically indistinguishable from the probe ($+0.002$ $[-0.003,+0.007]$). The interesting regime is conflict. There $\punk$ loses $0.159$ AUROC, maximum probability loses $0.152$ and falls to chance, and even a learned readout over the entire output distribution loses $0.059$. The frozen probe loses $0.038$ and transfers at $0.957$ on held-out scenes; the refit probe reaches $0.995$, which shows that a decoder can still read the evidence from the conflict state but does not by itself show that the no-hint decoder does.

The distinction between frozen and refit matters for the claim. The refit number answers ``is the evidence still linearly decodable?'' and the answer is yes. The frozen number answers ``does the no-hint linear-readout direction still rank the arms on scenes it has never seen?'' and the answer is yes, with a loss that is small in AUROC but large in geometry (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). We report the frozen number in the abstract and use the refit number only as an upper bound. Scene overlap does inflate the number, but not by much: scoring the same probe on its own fitting scenes gives $0.971$ against $0.957$ held out for this pair, and the gap is $0.002$--$0.035$ across models and hints. A $C$ grid from $3\times10^{-4}$ to $0.1$ moves the held-out value by at most $0.020$ (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}). The far-hue hint on both arms gives $0.958$ in 3B, $0.946$ in 7B and $0.935$ in LLaVA; the plausible colour gives $0.957$/$0.939$/$0.923$. Under a correct hint no signal collapses: the frozen probe reaches $0.990$ and $\punk$ $0.969$, ruling out an explanation in which the probe merely responds to the presence of a hint string.

The invariance is a statement about rank, not about the state. In probe-score units the present-minus-absent gap shrinks from $15.9$ to $7.7$ for the plausible hint and to $7.3$ for the far-hue hint, contractions to $0.485$ $[0.460,0.511]$ and $0.463$ $[0.438,0.488]$ respectively. Within-arm standard deviations are almost unchanged ($2.8$ and $3.1$, then $2.6$ and $2.9$), so in pooled-SD units the separation falls from $5.1$ to $2.6$. The contraction replicates in 7B ($0.416$ $[0.386,0.448]$ and $0.424$ $[0.393,0.457]$) and LLaVA ($0.361$ $[0.337,0.384]$ and $0.344$ $[0.324,0.365]$). This is a projection onto one direction, so by itself it cannot say whether the states moved together, shrank, or turned away from the probe. The next subsection measures the full vector and finds all three.

\subsection{Dilution and rotation in the full space}
\label{sec:geometry}

The probe-score gap of the previous paragraph is a projection onto a single direction, the unit no-hint mean difference $\wdir$. To separate length from direction we compute, for each condition, the full mean-difference vector $\Delta\mu=\bar h_{\text{present}}-\bar h_{\text{absent}}$ and report its norm relative to the no-hint norm, its angle to $\wdir$ (the principal angle between the two one-dimensional subspaces), its projection $\Delta\mu\cdot\wdir$, its length after whitening by the no-hint within-class covariance (Mahalanobis distance; ridge $1\%$ of the mean eigenvalue), and the coherence of the per-scene paired gaps $v_s=h_{\text{present},s}-h_{\text{absent},s}$, defined as $\|\bar v_s\|/\mathrm{rms}_s\|v_s\|$. All quantities carry scene-grouped bootstrap intervals ($500$ draws). The same estimator applied to the no-hint vector against itself gives ratio $1.00$ $[0.97,1.05]$ and an angle of $3^\circ$--$5^\circ$, which is the noise floor of the measurement.

The information is re-coded rather than deleted. Whitened by the no-hint within-class noise, the separation does not fall at all: Mahalanobis distance goes from $0.45$ to $0.52$/$0.49$ in 3B, $0.43$ to $0.55$/$0.53$ in 7B and $0.42$ to $0.63$/$0.62$ in LLaVA, and a readout refitted inside the condition reaches $0.995$--$0.999$. What does fall is agreement across scenes. Per-scene gaps keep most of their length ($78.5\to61.7$ in 3B, $155.4\to102.9$ in 7B, $108.4\to87.9$ in LLaVA) but stop pointing the same way: coherence drops from $0.60$ to $0.40$, from $0.70$ to $0.41$ and from $0.61$ to $0.45$. The aggregate vector is short for two reasons---each gap is shorter, and shorter gaps disagree in direction---and a fixed linear readout sees only the aggregate. The rank scale is more robust than the absolute scale: in raw projection units, across four models and three hinted conditions, the mean gap falls to $0.20$--$0.69$ of its no-hint value while the within-class spread along the same direction falls only to $0.62$--$0.83$, so $d'$ falls sub-proportionally to dilution. Quantitatively, across the $12$ model--condition cells, $d'_c/d'_0=1.42\,g_c+0.003$ with $R^2=0.918$, where $g_c=(\Delta\mu_c\cdot\wdir)/(\Delta\mu_0\cdot\wdir)$ is the along-axis projection ratio of Table~\ref{tab:geometry} (through the origin $1.425$, RMSE $0.060$, maximum residual $0.098$). The gain is stable within a model across hints---$1.25$ in 3B, $1.46$ in 7B, $1.40$ in LLaVA, $1.60$ in Gemma---and equals the reciprocal of the average spread contraction ($1/0.707$). Since $d'=\Delta\mu/\sigma$ is an identity, the empirical content is the near-constancy of that contraction: the rank-sensitivity loss is predictable from the mean-gap ratio with one per-model gain. Out of sample the per-model form holds: fitting on $11$ cells and predicting the twelfth has MAE $0.056$, against $0.179$ for a unit slope and $0.202$ for the training mean, and a scene bootstrap that re-estimates $\wdir$ in every draw puts the through-origin slope at $1.37$--$1.47$ (Table~\ref{tab:lawval}). The gain does not extrapolate across models: leaving one model out misses its cells by $0.02$ (7B), $0.02$ (LLaVA), $0.12$ (3B) and $0.12$ (Gemma), the two extreme gains, so a new model needs its own hinted cells before the law can be used on it. The gain is not shared across models, and it says nothing about the output head; Section~\ref{sec:sdt} tests whether the same dilution reaches the model's own gate. This is the representational fact behind the output collapse: $\punk$ and maximum probability are absolute, scale-dependent readings of a representation that has been diluted and rotated, whereas a rank-based readout keeps most of its accuracy because the ordering survives. Neither quantity orders the models the way their behaviour does (7B dilutes most yet adopts hints least; LLaVA rotates most and adopts most), so the geometry is a shared consequence of the prompt rather than a readout of credulity. The measurement is descriptive: it establishes that a hint does not remove visual evidence from the state, and it bounds what a fixed direction can recover, but it does not identify the weights that apply the transformation.

In Gemma-3-4B-it a conflicting hint shortens the evidence vector to $0.529$ $[0.490,0.567]$ (hint a) and $0.543$ $[0.502,0.580]$ (hint b) of its no-hint length and turns it $29^\circ$/$24^\circ$ away from $\wdir$, five to six times its own noise floor ($1.00$ $[0.94,1.06]$, $4.5^\circ$). The internal geometry therefore generalises across three language-model families, while the output-space collapse that motivates a frozen gate appears in the Qwen2/2.5 and Phi-3 readouts but not in Gemma; it is readout-specific rather than family-general. In Gemma the gate's margin over $\punk$ is smaller ($0.964$ against $0.891$) but still present. The decision-level decomposition is model-specific as well: on the fixed no-hint axis Gemma shows a criterion shift with no detectable sensitivity loss ($\Delta d'=-0.44$ $[-0.88,+0.17]$, $\Delta c=+1.25$ $[+0.72,+1.26]$, scene bootstrap), where 3B loses sensitivity ($3.51\to1.45$). What survives in every backbone tested here is the geometry, not the signal-detection profile.

\subsection{Dilution, not a criterion shift}
\label{sec:sdt}

A natural reading of Table~\ref{tab:behaviour} is that the hint lowers the refusal threshold. Signal detection separates this from a loss of sensitivity. Using the out-of-fold probe score as the evidence axis, we compute $d'$ and criterion $c$ for each condition (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:sdt}), and fit the psychometric model $\mathrm{logit}\,P(\text{refuse})=b_0+az+\sum_k d_k(b_k+a_kz)$ over the 1{,}827 item--condition observations. The fit is run twice: on the \emph{fixed} axis, $z$ is the no-hint state of the same item; on the \emph{own} axis, $z$ is the condition's own state projected on the same no-hint direction.

Under conflict, sensitivity falls from $3.51$ to $1.45$ ($\Delta d'=-2.13$ $[-2.69,-1.66]$), while the criterion does not move reliably in this model ($\Delta c=+0.16$ $[-0.18,+0.39]$). A correct hint leaves both unchanged ($\Delta d'=+0.31$ $[-0.36,+0.93]$). The behavioural effect is bidirectional---conflict raises refusal on present items and lowers it on absent items---and a criterion shift moves both arms in the same direction, so the criterion account cannot produce the pattern.

On the fixed axis the conflict slope is $-0.171$ against $-0.719$ (change $+0.548$ $[0.439,0.774]$; likelihood-ratio $178.2$, $df=2$, $p<10^{-38}$), a $4.2$-fold flattening. Most of that is the axis. The hint halves the separation on the probe's direction (Section~\ref{sec:geometry} shows this to be dilution plus rotation), so a one-unit change on the fixed axis corresponds to about half a unit on the direction the decision reads. Applying the condition's own slope to the diluted axis predicts a $2.1$-fold flattening; the remainder comes from imperfect coupling between the pre- and post-hint projections (correlation $0.86$). When $z$ is taken from the conflict state itself, the slope change is $+0.160$ $[0.013,0.429]$ (LR $6.4$, $p=0.041$): in its own coordinates the decision is $22\%$ flatter. That residual is small but nonzero, and we report it as a real sensitivity change rather than absorbed by the dilution of the axis.

The sensitivity collapse replicates in all three models; the criterion does not. Its shift is null in 3B, negative in 7B ($-0.54$ $[-0.73,-0.35]$) and positive in LLaVA ($+1.00$ $[+0.66,+1.33]$). A uniform lowering of the refusal threshold predicts a common direction, so the cross-model pattern argues against a criterion account. It also warns against over-reading $c$: the equal-variance estimate is biased when $d'$ changes, which is why we treat the consistent $d'$ loss, not the sign of $\Delta c$, as the invariant. Under a correct hint $d'$ never falls ($\Delta d'=+0.31$, $+0.50$, $+0.32$ for 3B/7B/LLaVA), so the loss is specific to conflict. The flattening is $4.2$-fold in 3B; we do not use the word ``fourfold'' for the other models, where the fixed-axis changes are $+0.332$ (LR $164.8$) and $+0.286$ (LR $40.8$). On each condition's own axis those fall to $+0.113$ $[0.025,0.232]$ (LR $22.0$) and $+0.053$ $[-0.057,0.185]$ (LR $1.6$, n.s.), residual gain losses of $24\%$ and $12\%$.

Across models, the sensitivity loss scales with the geometric dilution. For the probe that relation is algebraic, since $d'$ is its own gap over its own spread; to test it on behaviour we replace the probe score with the logit of $p(\unknown)$ and compute the behavioural $d'$ over present and absent items, whose spread is measured on the output signal and is free to vary. Over the eight hinted cells (four models $\times$ two conflicting hues; the correct-hint cell has no hinted absent arm) the behavioural ratio follows the geometric one, $d'_{\text{out},c}/d'_{\text{out},0}=0.97\,g_c+0.12$ with $R^2=0.57$ (through the origin $1.27$; scene-bootstrap slope $[0.72,1.24]$), and an argmax-rate $d'$ gives $1.47$ through the origin ($R^2=0.51$; Table~\ref{tab:lawbeh}). The constant is model-specific: averaged over the two hues, the behavioural ratio is $0.92$ of the geometric one in 3B and $1.43$, $1.62$ and $1.40$ in 7B, LLaVA and Gemma, which lose less behavioural sensitivity than the projection on $\wdir$ loses separation. Dilution therefore predicts the direction and the order of magnitude of the behavioural sensitivity loss up to a model-specific factor that the decision stage sets, not the evidence axis. The fit is descriptive, not predictive: leave-one-cell-out prediction has MAE $0.100$ against $0.125$ for predicting the training mean and $0.121$ for a unit slope, and leaving a model out misses its cells by $0.02$ (7B), $0.07$ (LLaVA), $0.21$ (3B) and $0.25$ (Gemma). What transfers across models is the linear form and an order-of-magnitude scale; the constant has to be measured inside the model, and with two hinted cells per model it is fitted, not predicted.

\paragraph{Removal is not direction-specific.}
Sufficiency does not imply necessity, and the raw necessity test is biased. Projecting out $\wdir$ lowers absent refusal by $0.214$ (3B), $0.211$ (7B) and $0.102$ (LLaVA), but $\wdir$ carries $5.4\times$ more absolute projection energy than a random direction on average (mean fraction $0.055$ against $0.019$, $0.004$ and $0.008$; $5.3\times$ in LLaVA), so the raw comparison pits directions that remove different amounts of the state. The same asymmetry holds in the two non-Qwen models: the raw effect is $-0.002$ in Gemma and $-0.057$ in Phi, with energy ratios $5.9\times$ and $7.3\times$. We therefore match the removed energy in both directions (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Scaling $\wdir$ down to the energy of a random direction reduces its effect to $-0.073$ $[-0.096,-0.052]$ in 3B and $-0.018$ $[-0.031,-0.008]$ in 7B. Scaling a random direction up to the energy of $\wdir$ is null in 3B ($+0.010$ $[-0.004,+0.025]$) but not in 7B ($-0.044$ $[-0.063,-0.027]$), where it is larger than the matched effect of $\wdir$. The class-mean and first-PC directions, scaled to the same energy, leave refusal unchanged in 7B ($+0.004$ $[-0.011,+0.019]$) and raise it in 3B ($+0.083$ $[+0.052,+0.111]$). In LLaVA the gate is already near the floor (base refusal $0.138$), and there the matched effect of $\wdir$ ($-0.011$ $[-0.021,-0.004]$) is smaller than that of a matched random direction ($-0.029$ $[-0.046,-0.013]$) and of the matched class-mean direction ($-0.063$ $[-0.088,-0.042]$). In Gemma the raw ablation is already null (base $0.439$, $\wdir$ raw $0.437$, $-0.002$ $[-0.006,0.000]$), so there is no removal effect to attribute; matching leaves it null ($\wdir{\to}r$: $-0.002$ $[-0.006,0.000]$; $r{\to}\wdir$: $-0.002$ $[-0.010,0.004]$), whereas class-mean and first-PC directions at matched energy raise refusal by $+0.021$ $[0.010,0.033]$ each. $\wdir$ therefore carries no removal effect at Gemma's head even though injecting it moves the gate, consistent with a movable but non-specific readout. In Phi the raw ablation ($-0.057$ $[-0.079,-0.036]$) falls to $-0.013$ $[-0.025,-0.004]$ when scaled to a random direction's energy, while a random direction scaled to $\wdir$'s energy raises refusal by $+0.048$ $[0.029,0.069]$; class-mean and first-PC directions at matched energy lower it by $0.157$ $[-0.193,-0.125]$ and $0.249$ $[-0.291,-0.211]$. The direction that works best when injected is therefore not the one whose removal matters most. No direction is necessary in a direction-specific sense: most of the raw removal effect is removed energy; the residual matched effect of $\wdir$ is at most $0.073$, and in 7B, LLaVA and Phi a matched random direction moves refusal at least as much (in Phi, more and in the opposite direction). What survives is sufficiency. Injecting $\wdir$ at the readout moves the gate monotonically, including when the dose is scaled to each prompt's own final-state norm (absent refusal $1.000\to0.134$ in 3B and $1.000\to0.073$ in 7B between $-0.25$ and $+0.25$), so the dose--response is not an artifact of a fixed absolute norm. We describe $\wdir$ as an efficient readout direction for evidence availability, not as an identified circuit element. The efficiency is model-specific: $\wdir$ is $1.7$--$3.1\times$ the mean random effect at the head in the Qwen-family models and Phi, but $0.55\times$ in Gemma (Appendix~\ref{app:site}), so we do not extend it to all five.

\paragraph{Localisation.}
Patching answers where restoring the unpolluted state reopens the gate. In 3B, patching at layer 24 ($67\%$ depth) recovers $22\%$ of the lost refusal and $\punk$, and layer 32 ($89\%$) recovers $80\%$. In 7B the corresponding values are $0\%$, $69\%$ and $90\%$ at layers 18, 24 and 26 ($64$, $86$, $93\%$); in LLaVA they are $36\%$, $80\%$ and $96\%$. Recovery is concentrated late in every model, but the transition depth follows the architecture: in Gemma it is $12\%$ at layer 16 ($47\%$ depth), $90\%$ at layer 24 ($71\%$) and $101\%$ at layer 30 ($88\%$), and in Phi $31\%$ at layer 14 ($44\%$), $97\%$ at layer 22 ($69\%$), $102\%$ at layer 28 ($88\%$) and $101\%$ at layer 30 ($94\%$). A shuffled-source control recovers as much or more (3B L32 $0.927$; 7B L26 $0.965$; LLaVA L26 $0.842$; Gemma L24/L30 $1.04$/$1.04$; Phi L22/L28 $1.07$/$1.05$), so the restored state carries a category-level ``no evidence'' status, not a sample-specific code, and the comparison cannot separate evidence from a generic no-hint prior. A same-image control does separate the question: with the image fixed, patching the image's own absent-question state at L32 raises refusal from $0.521$ to $0.839$, whereas its present-question state drives refusal to $0.008$ (paired difference $+0.83$ $[0.79,0.87]$), with the same contrast in 7B ($+0.84$), LLaVA ($+0.74$), Gemma ($+0.70$/$+0.77$ at L24/L30) and Phi ($+0.71$/$+0.77$ at L22/L30). The late state therefore encodes a question-conditioned answer/refuse policy. A no-op patch that pastes the same pass's own state returns the base distribution exactly (argmax agreement $1.00$ and maximum probability change $0$ at both probed layers in all five models), whereas a zero paste changes the argmax in $10.7\%$/$17.2\%$ of absent items in 3B, $38.7\%$/$6.1\%$ in 7B, $72.4\%$/$99.6\%$ in LLaVA, $90.0\%$/$80.5\%$ in Gemma and $76.6\%$/$91.6\%$ in Phi. The hook is therefore active and the pasted vector, not the instrumentation, drives the change, so the recovery profile can be read quantitatively; the caveat is that pasting a foreign state still shifts the input distribution. The claim is that the gate is written late, not that we have identified the computation.

LLaVA-OneVision-7B provides the strongest available generality test. It uses a different vision encoder and training recipe but shares the Qwen language backbone. Its present-item refusal under conflict is $0.004$, against $0.556$ in Qwen-7B: the same hint is much more effective on this model. Yet the recovery profile has the same shape and depth, so a model that is easier to fool does not have an earlier gate; its gate is pushed further open at the same place.

\subsection{External validation and task form}

On VizWiz \citep{gurari2018vizwiz} (462 items; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:vizwiz}) conflict lowers unanswerable refusal from $0.704$ to $0.636$ and raises answerable refusal from $0.099$ to $0.193$ while dropping accuracy from $0.580$ to $0.451$. The frozen probe transfers at $0.827$/$0.720$, above maximum probability but below $\punk$ ($0.878$/$0.793$): natural unanswerable questions fail for heterogeneous reasons, and the hint does not attack the refusal gate. On VQA-v2 colour questions the ordering survives but calibration does not: the score shifts from $+6.93$ to $+2.10$ ($r=0.54$), and maximum probability has lower error at $50\%$ coverage ($0.007$ vs.\ $0.087$). A task-form swap (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:task}) leaves the gate intact but collapses the output readout mainly for colour ($\punk$ loses $0.159$, vs.\ $0.023$ and $0.040$), and the gate's advantage tracks that damage.

Two further replications bound the scope. A second stimulus set built on PASCAL VOC 2012 segmentation ($180$ images, automatic labels, same 3B checkpoint) reproduces the behaviour, the frozen transfer, the geometry and the gate: conflict lowers absent refusal from $0.894$ to $0.644$, the frozen probe reaches $0.925$/$0.950$, the evidence vector shortens to $0.41$/$0.52$ and turns $35^\circ$/$33^\circ$, and at $25\%$ answered coverage the gate cuts fabrication from $0.367$ to $0.144$ (Appendix~\ref{app:voc}). Phi-3.5-vision replicates the dissociation in a third language-model family with a third vision encoder and is the extreme case: a refit reaches $0.998$ while the frozen probe falls to $0.685$ after an $86^\circ$ rotation, and the gate cuts fabrication from $0.562$ to $0.285$ at $25\%$ coverage (Appendix~\ref{app:phi}).

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the hint does.}
The hint does not erase the evidence; it dilutes and rotates it, and changes a late decision about whether to report it. A fresh readout on the hinted state still ranks the arms at $0.989$--$0.999$, but the no-hint direction loses more than half its separation, and that loss decomposes into a shorter mean difference ($0.39$--$0.64$ of its length) and a systematic turn away from the direction ($24^\circ$--$71^\circ$), with the per-scene gaps also falling out of agreement in the Qwen-family models and, most sharply, in Phi-3.5 ($0.39\to0.03$). A direction added at the readout moves the decision. The most parsimonious account is a readout that reads a fixed direction, fed by an evidence-availability representation computed upstream whose content is preserved but re-coded by the assertion, with the abstention decision written late. It is not yet a mechanism: the steering is at the readout, energy-matched removal shows that no single direction is necessary, and the geometry analysis bounds what a fixed direction can recover without identifying the weights that apply the transformation.

\paragraph{When the gate helps.}
In the constructed regime the hint attacks the refusal gate and $\punk$ is corrupted; there the cross-fitted probe buys $2.2$--$2.5\times$ in 3B and LLaVA, $9.0\times$ in Gemma and $2.0\times$ in Phi-3.5 at a quarter of items answered, and a null margin in 7B, though the category-matched control shows that part of this margin is specific to the change of question between the two arms (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}). On naturally unanswerable images the failure is heterogeneous and $\punk$ is already good, so the probe adds little. The score is an evidence detector, not a calibrated per-item predictor of credulity: it predicts absent-item refusal in three models but adoption only in Gemma (Appendix~\ref{app:validity}). A practical system should use the internal gate when it suspects prompt pollution, operate it by coverage rather than a fixed threshold, and fall back to calibrated confidence when evidence is present.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The benchmark is small ($261$ images) but densely observed. Absent categories are sampled without requiring scene plausibility, and ``absent'' is annotation-level; a co-occurrence score for the absent category against the image's own objects predicts no-hint refusal (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}), so the gate partly tracks scene fit, though evidence detection survives within both strata ($\punk$ AUROC $\ge0.976$) and the effect survives a control for category frequency. On the main absent arm a yes/no presence probe gives mean $p(\text{yes})=0.046$ and judges $2.7\%$ of items present above $0.5$; restricting to the $254$ items it judges absent leaves refusal under conflict at $0.531$ (against $0.521$ on all items), hint adoption at $0.457$, and probe AUROC at $0.999$. LLaVA's own presence readout is weak (mean $p(\text{yes})=0.403$), so for LLaVA absence rests on the annotation, though its strict subset gives the same picture (refusal $0.104$, adoption $0.891$, probe $1.000$). A category-matched control that holds the question and the asserted colour fixed across the label reproduces the output collapse, the in-condition decodability and the geometry, but reduces the frozen probe's margin over $\punk$ from $+0.123$/$+0.142$/$+0.142$ to $+0.050$/$+0.002$/$-0.015$ in 3B/7B/LLaVA and removes its fabrication advantage in 7B and LLaVA; the same control leaves Gemma's margin at $-0.009$ and keeps Phi's positive only because its own signal is below chance, so the deployment numbers are an upper bound for a setting in which the question changes between arms. The main prompts offer an explicit \unknown\ option and a bracketed hint. Removing the \unknown\ option removes abstention by construction (refusal $0$) and raises hint adoption from $0.467$ to $0.935$ in 3B and $0.123$ to $0.977$ in 7B; phrasing the hint as a sentence rather than a bracket restores refusal to $0.958$/$0.989$ and all but eliminates hint adoption ($0.038$/$0.004$), while accuracy under the wrong hint rises from $0.383$ to $0.594$ in 3B and $0.249$ to $0.498$ in 7B. LLaVA moves the same way from a lower baseline: sentence phrasing raises conflict refusal from $0.088$ to $0.559$, cuts adoption from $0.904$ to $0.333$ and raises wrong-hint accuracy from $0.360$ to $0.548$. Gemma and Phi-3.5 follow the same pattern: removing the \unknown\ option raises adoption from $0.579$/$0.663$ to $0.789$/$0.985$, sentence phrasing raises conflict refusal from $0.402$/$0.322$ to $0.851$/$0.877$ and cuts adoption to $0.038$/$0.088$, and wrong-hint accuracy rises from $0.241$/$0.080$ to $0.559$/$0.276$. The behavioural conflict effect is therefore specific to an available abstention token and, in Qwen, to where the assertion sits: single-turn user-message sentences largely suppress it, while a system-message assertion or a two-turn dialogue restores it; in Gemma the ordering reverses and only the bracketed form produces substantial adoption. The internal dilution and rotation appear in every form and every model we tested, so we do not attribute the geometry to the bracket (Appendix~\ref{app:sixform}). A query-specificity control shows that rotation is not triggered by conflict alone: in 3B a neutral sentence with no object and no colour turns the evidence axis $42.7^\circ$ $[41.4,44.2]$, as far as the conflicting assertion about the queried category ($44.2^\circ$). The displacement is nevertheless query-linked: in all three backbones a sentence about the queried category that holds one colour fixed across arms is nearly collinear with the conflict axis ($\cos 0.976$/$0.988$/$0.997$ in 3B/7B/Gemma) and shortens the axis to $0.604$/$0.596$/$0.877$, against $0.804$/$0.691$/$0.897$ for an assertion about another object and $0.822$/$0.730$/$1.000$ for a neutral sentence; the conflict arms, which also change the asserted colour, shorten it further to $0.589$/$0.568$/$0.885$. We therefore read the conflict effect as the extreme of a graded, query-relevance-dependent compression, with the colour change contributing a smaller additional increment. We do not claim the behavioural effect for readouts without \unknown. The mechanistic claims are strongest for colour. The probe requires white-box states. Energy-matched removal shows no direction-specific necessity, so we claim readout-level sufficiency, not a circuit. To break the confound between architecture and training recipe we added two backbones outside the Qwen family. Gemma-3-4B-it (Gemma-3 with a SigLIP encoder) abstains without hints ($0.843$ of absent items) and is overridden by conflict ($0.402$/$0.475$ refusal, adoption $0.579$/$0.502$). Its evidence vector dilutes to $0.53$ and rotates $24^\circ$--$29^\circ$, its two hinted colours coincide at $\cos=0.971$, and its frozen probe transfers at $0.964$/$0.970$, so the geometry replicates outside the Qwen family; its output confidence, however, barely degrades ($\punk$ AUROC $0.961\to0.891$, maximum probability $0.627\to0.701$). Phi-3.5-vision (Phi-3 with a CLIP encoder) abstains ($0.897$), is overridden ($0.322$/$0.425$ refusal, adoption $0.663$/$0.567$), dilutes its evidence vector to $0.052$/$0.149$ and rotates it $86^\circ$/$74^\circ$, and its output signal collapses as in the Qwen family ($\punk$ $0.939\to0.385$/$0.458$); its frozen probe falls to $0.685$/$0.675$ while a refit reaches $0.998$ (Appendix~\ref{app:phi}). The rank--scale dissociation is therefore about the representation; the collapse of the native output signal appears in the Qwen2/2.5 and Phi-3 readouts but not in Gemma, so it is readout-specific rather than a property of one language-model family. SmolVLM-Instruct (Idefics3 with a SmolLM2 language model) remains a boundary case with no abstention behaviour at all (refusal $0$ in every cell), so it cannot test the gate. The geometry analysis is descriptive: it establishes dilution, rotation and loss of cross-scene coherence, and bounds what a fixed direction recovers, but a rotated yet fully decodable axis is compatible with several implementations and we do not identify which. We also tested two candidate laws from geometry to behaviour and rejected both: a condition-invariant slope (likelihood-ratio $37.8$, $df=5$, $p=4.2\times10^{-7}$ in 3B) and isotropic contraction, under which the within-class spread would shrink with the mean gap (across four models and three hinted conditions the gap ratio is $0.20$--$0.69$ but the spread ratio is $0.62$--$0.83$, log--log slope $0.006$, $R^2\approx0$). What survives is weaker but quantitative: for the fixed readout the rank-sensitivity loss is proportional to the mean-gap ratio, $d'_c/d'_0=1.42\,g_c+0.003$ ($R^2=0.918$, leave-one-cell-out MAE $0.056$), with a per-model gain of $1.25$--$1.60$ in the four fitted models that is stable across hints (Phi-3.5, out of sample, has a gain of $1.13$ and RMSE $0.096$). This is a within-readout regularity, not a law from geometry to the model's own confidence, which Gemma dissociates. Input resolution is not the driver, since moving the 3B model from its native $589{,}824$ to $262{,}144$ pixels changes absent refusal only from $0.521$ to $0.556$ and leaves the frozen probe at AUROC $0.997$. The conformal risk bound is not usable at $130$ calibration scenes for most models: with the answered subset in both numerator and denominator, no family-wise $5\%$ level exists in any model and a family-wise $10\%$ level exists only for Gemma in $4/20$ splits, while the pointwise $10\%$ bound holds in $7/20$, $11/20$, $7/20$, $20/20$ and $0/20$ splits, so we withdraw the general certificate claim. The invariance is rank invariance, not calibration invariance. Labels were verified by a single annotator on contact sheets; we did not run a second-annotator agreement study.

\section{Conclusion}

A conflicting hint does not make a vision-language model blind to the image. It dilutes and rotates the internal evidence axis---shortening the present--absent mean difference to $0.05$--$0.64$ of its no-hint length and turning it $24^\circ$--$86^\circ$ off the direction a no-hint readout uses---and overrides a late decision to abstain. The classes remain decodable (a refit recovers at least $0.989$) and the ranking survives on held-out scenes, though after the largest rotation it falls to $0.685$; the scale and the alignment do not, which is why in the Qwen-family and Phi models the model's own confidence collapses while a freshly fitted readout does not. The override is localised to the last third of the network in all five white-box models, with the transition at $69$--$94\%$ depth; the behavioural override and its internal geometry replicate across five models from three language-model families (Qwen, Gemma and Phi) and three vision encoders; and the direction is bidirectionally controllable at the readout where we tested it, while energy-matched removal shows that it is not necessary. The practical consequence is a gate fitted without model training that cuts fabrication by $2.2$--$2.5\times$ in 3B and LLaVA, $9.0\times$ in Gemma and $2.0\times$ in Phi at a quarter of items answered (null in 7B), with the boundary that on naturally unanswerable images $\punk$ is already as good; we do not claim a distribution-free certificate. The scientific question is what applies the dilution and the rotation, and whether a gate can be trained to ignore assertions that the image contradicts.

\section{Scene-held-out transfer and regularisation sensitivity}
\label{app:heldout}

The headline frozen numbers fit the probe on the no-hint states of four scene folds and score the fifth fold's hinted states, so no scene contributes to both fitting and evaluation. The protocol matters: scoring the probe on the same scenes it was fitted on gives $0.971$ for the far-hue hint in 3B against $0.958$ $[0.939,0.974]$ held out, and the inflation is $0.002$--$0.035$ across the four models and three hint conditions (largest for LLaVA, $0.958\to0.923$; Gemma-3-4B inflates by $0.010$--$0.019$, $0.982\to0.964$ for the plausible hint). Scene overlap therefore helped the earlier numbers estimate, but it does not account for the ranking: the held-out probe still separates the conflict arms far above chance and within $0.04$ of its fitting-scene value.

The regularisation is fixed at $C=0.1$ for every reported probe number. Sweeping $C\in\{3\times10^{-4},10^{-3},3\times10^{-3},10^{-2},0.1\}$ under the held-out protocol moves the conflict AUROC by at most $0.020$ (LLaVA, plausible hint, $0.906$--$0.926$), $0.012$ in 3B and $0.016$ in 7B; for the far-hue hint the spread is at most $0.016$ in every model. No choice of $C$ changes the ordering---conflict below no hint, correct at or near ceiling---or the size of the output-signal collapse, which does not depend on the probe at all. We report the fixed $C=0.1$ rather than the grid-selected value so that the headline number is not chosen on the evaluation set.

\section{Controls for text priors and strict absence}
\label{app:controls}

A gray image with the same prompts and dimensions yields probe AUROC $0.849$/$0.981$/$0.930$ under no/conflicting/correct hints, and $\punk$ $0.700$/$0.325$/$0.433$. Text alone carries some category-to-colour prior, enough to reach $0.85$ on the no-hint pair. But the direction learned from gray images transfers across hints at only $0.540$/$0.603$, whereas the real-image direction transfers at $0.958$/$0.990$ on held-out scenes (Table~\ref{tab:signals}). The real-image direction is shared across prompts; the text-prior direction is not.

Category-matched pairs hold the question fixed, which the main design does not: there the present and absent arms query different categories, so a present--absent separation can be carried by the question and the asserted colour as well as by the image. In the control, each present image is paired with a different image in which the same category is annotated absent, the question is identical, and the same colour is asserted in both arms (the category's most frequent val colour, which is never the present image's gold), so neither the question nor the hint can predict the label. There are $260$ pairs over $30$ categories. Table~\ref{tab:catmatched} reports the result.

The output collapse, the in-condition decodability and the geometry all survive. $\punk$ falls from $0.983$ to $0.817$ in 3B, from $0.975$ to $0.820$ in 7B, from $0.989$ to $0.870$ in LLaVA, from $0.930$ to $0.896$ in Gemma and from $0.891$ to $0.446$ in Phi, and maximum-probability AUROC from $0.952$ to $0.699$, from $0.967$ to $0.768$, from $0.949$ to $0.510$, from $0.904$ to $0.783$ and from $0.903$ to $0.357$; refitting recovers $0.975$, $0.978$, $0.983$, $0.933$ and $0.950$; and the present--absent mean difference shortens to $0.565$, $0.394$, $0.586$, $0.546$ and $0.547$ of its no-hint length and turns $39.6^\circ$, $45.9^\circ$, $65.9^\circ$, $23.8^\circ$ and $81.5^\circ$, against $0.523$/$0.384$/$0.602$ and $38.5^\circ$/$47.4^\circ$/$70.7^\circ$ for the Qwen-family plausible hint on the main pair (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}). The Phi case also separates the two sources of dilution: on the main pair the vector keeps only $0.052$ of its length, but within a fixed question it keeps $0.547$ while still turning $82^\circ$, so much of the main-pair shortening is carried by the change of queried category, not by the hint alone. What does not survive is the size of the frozen transfer. With the question and the hint held constant across the label, the no-hint probe scores $0.867$, $0.823$ and $0.855$, and its margin over $\punk$ is $+0.050$ $[0.028,0.077]$, $+0.002$ $[-0.025,0.045]$ and $-0.015$ $[-0.062,0.025]$, against $+0.123$, $+0.142$ and $+0.142$ on the main pair; in 7B and LLaVA the margin is indistinguishable from zero, and in LLaVA the point estimate is negative. Gemma follows: frozen $0.887$, margin $-0.009$ $[-0.032,0.011]$. Phi is the exception, with frozen $0.592$ and margin $+0.145$ $[0.063,0.232]$, but this is a comparison against an own signal at $0.446$, below chance, and the probe itself is far from the $0.950$ refit; it is the least bad score, not a good one. A substantial part of the main design's frozen transfer is therefore carried by the change of queried category and asserted colour between the arms, not by the evidence alone. The refit probe is not exposed to this confound, because it is fitted where question and hint are constant, and the geometry is computed within the pair; both remain. Grouping folds by image instead of category removes donor-image reuse ($157$ images serve as both a present image and a negative) and leaves the frozen and refit values within $0.01$ ($0.876$/$0.984$ in 3B, $0.833$/$0.980$ in 7B, $0.869$/$0.989$ in LLaVA).

At the same $25\%$ answered coverage ($130$ of $520$ items), the frozen probe's fabrication rate on the conflict pair is $0.185$ (3B), $0.154$ (7B), $0.146$ (LLaVA), $0.131$ (Gemma) and $0.438$ (Phi), against $0.254$/$0.154$/$0.054$/$0.115$/$0.446$ for $\punk$ and $0.346$/$0.169$/$0.600$/$0.254$/$0.592$ for maximum probability. In 3B the gate still beats the model's own signal within-question, but by $0.069$ instead of $0.115$ on the main pair; in 7B the probe and $\punk$ coincide; in LLaVA and Gemma the probe is worse than $\punk$; and in Phi it is better by only $0.008$ at this coverage while ranking better ($0.592$ against $0.446$). The 7B point is weakly informative because it answers only $26\%$ of matched conflict items, so $25\%$ of all items selects $95\%$ of its answers; LLaVA answers $90\%$, so its point is informative and unfavourable, and Phi's $25\%$ point selects $34\%$ of items. The main-design gate advantage is therefore partly an arm-diagnostic advantage, and we report both. Excluding the $17$ of $260$ negatives that the presence probe judges present above $0.5$ leaves this unchanged: $\punk$ is $0.992$ without hints and $0.851$ under conflict, and maximum probability $0.692$ and $0.473$. The main absent arm is checked the same way: per-item presence probabilities on all $261$ absent images give mean $p(\text{yes})=0.046$, and $7$ items are judged present above $0.5$. On the remaining $254$, refusal under the conflicting hint is $0.531$ (against $0.521$ on all items), refusal with no hint is $0.921$, hint adoption is $0.457$, and the evidence probe reaches AUROC $0.999$; the strict-absence restriction therefore does not change the main result. For LLaVA the presence check is much weaker (mean $p(\text{yes})=0.403$, $73$ items above $0.5$), so its yes/no readout is not a reliable absence detector; on the $183$ items it judges absent, conflict refusal moves only from $0.088$ to $0.104$, adoption is $0.891$ and the probe remains at $1.000$. For LLaVA, ``absent'' therefore rests on the annotation rather than on the probe.

\paragraph{A model without an abstention behaviour.}
We also ran the main battery on SmolVLM-Instruct (2B; Idefics3 vision encoder, SmolLM2 language model) as a non-Qwen backbone. It never emits \unknown: refusal is $0$ in all six cells, and it adopts the stated colour on $0.985$ (hint a) and $0.989$ (hint b) of absent items, so there is no behavioural gate to study and we do not use it as a replication. Its final state nonetheless separates present from absent items, at probe AUROC $0.989$ with no hint and $0.993$ under the conflicting hint ($p(\text{known})$ $0.921$ and $0.598$; maximum probability $0.877$ and $0.257$). A decodable evidence axis can therefore exist in a model that never abstains. Because the conflict-pair probe for this model could also pick up hint--image mismatch rather than evidence, we treat this as a boundary observation about the axis, not as evidence about the gate.

\paragraph{Scene fit of the absent category.}
The absent category is sampled without regard to whether it fits the scene, so abstention could track implausibility rather than missing evidence. For each item we score the absent category by its COCO train co-occurrence with the categories annotated in that image, $s=\mathrm{mean}_c\,P(\text{absent}\mid c)/P(\text{absent})$, and split at the median (Table~\ref{tab:scenefit}). The score predicts no-hint refusal in every model, so the absent arm does carry a scene-fit component: the better-fitting half refuses less by $0.116$ $[0.047,0.185]$ in 3B, $0.069$ $[0.023,0.115]$ in 7B and $0.132$ $[0.039,0.224]$ in LLaVA. Category frequency is a rival explanation, since frequent categories are also refused less in 7B ($0.073$ $[0.025,0.128]$) and LLaVA ($0.100$ $[0.008,0.195]$), though not detectably in 3B ($0.062$ $[-0.014,0.133]$). The two scores are only weakly related (Spearman $\rho=0.23$). Fitting no-hint refusal on both jointly with a scene bootstrap, scene fit remains negative in all three models, at $-0.81$ $[-1.26,-0.37]$ (3B), $-0.68$ $[-1.24,-0.29]$ (7B) and $-0.44$ $[-0.78,-0.16]$ (LLaVA) per standard deviation, while frequency is negative only in 7B ($-0.45$ $[-0.98,-0.14]$) and indistinguishable from zero in 3B ($-0.29$ $[-0.76,+0.11]$) and LLaVA ($-0.01$ $[-0.34,+0.37]$). The scene-fit difference is also present within each frequency half (Table~\ref{tab:scenefit2x2}). It does not account for the evidence signal. Within each half $\punk$ still separates present from absent images, at AUROC $0.988$--$0.998$ in 3B, $0.976$--$0.991$ in 7B and $0.986$--$0.995$ in LLaVA, and the probe at $0.992$--$0.999$, $0.989$--$1.000$ and $0.993$--$0.997$. Under the conflicting hint the stratum difference is not significant in 7B ($-0.047$ $[-0.124,+0.037]$) or LLaVA ($+0.024$ $[-0.045,+0.093]$) and remains in 3B ($-0.165$ $[-0.287,-0.050]$), where hint adoption is $0.157$ $[0.042,0.280]$ higher in the better-fitting half. The same conclusion therefore holds in both strata: plausibility moves the level of the gate, not the ranking of evidence.

\paragraph{Prompt format.}
The main battery offers an explicit \unknown\ option and a bracketed hint, which could overstate abstention or hint uptake. We rerun the absent arm three ways in all five models (Table~\ref{tab:promptfmt}). Removing the \unknown\ option makes abstention impossible by construction (refusal is $0$ in every condition) and raises adoption of the plausible hint from $0.467$ to $0.935$ in 3B, from $0.123$ to $0.977$ in 7B, from $0.904$ to $0.992$ in LLaVA, from $0.579$ to $0.789$ in Gemma and from $0.663$ to $0.985$ in Phi-3.5. Phrasing the hint as a sentence rather than a bracket leaves refusal under conflict at $0.958$/$0.989$ in 3B/7B, raises it from $0.088$ to $0.559$ in LLaVA, from $0.402$ to $0.851$ in Gemma and from $0.322$ to $0.877$ in Phi-3.5, while reducing hint adoption to $0.038$/$0.004$/$0.333$/$0.038$/$0.088$; accuracy under the wrong hint rises from $0.383$ to $0.594$ in 3B, from $0.249$ to $0.498$ in 7B, from $0.360$ to $0.548$ in LLaVA, from $0.241$ to $0.559$ in Gemma and from $0.080$ to $0.276$ in Phi-3.5. In Qwen the behavioural conflict effect is thus tied to the availability of an abstention token and to where the assertion sits: a single-turn sentence in the user message largely suppresses it, while an assertion in the system message or in a two-turn dialogue restores it. Gemma-3-4B reverses the ordering: the bracketed form is the only one with substantial adoption ($0.502$ vs.\ $0.004$--$0.119$), and \texttt{system} and \texttt{dialogue} suppress it. What is not an artifact of the bracketed prefix is the geometry: dilution and rotation of the evidence axis appear in all six forms in all three models, and a probe fitted on the bracketed form transfers at AUROC $0.95$--$0.999$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sixform}).

\paragraph{Input resolution.}
LLaVA runs at a different input resolution from the Qwen models, so we rerun the 3B battery at $262{,}144$ and $589{,}824$ pixels. At $262{,}144$, absent refusal is $0.908$ with no hint and $0.556$ under the plausible hint, hint adoption is $0.441$, accuracy under the wrong hint is $0.433$, and the frozen probe reaches AUROC $0.997$; at $589{,}824$ the values are $0.904$, $0.521$, $0.467$, $0.383$ and $1.000$, matching the native run. Resolution moves the level by at most $0.05$ and does not produce the conflict effect.

\section{Prompt form: is the effect an artifact of the bracket?}
\label{app:sixform}

The main battery states the assertion as a bracketed \texttt{Hint:} prefix, so
the conflict effect could in principle be a property of the prefix rather than
of the assertion. We reran the absent and sufficient arms in six forms on the
same $261$ scenes in both Qwen sizes and in Gemma-3-4B-it: the bracketed form
(\texttt{std}), a
colleague report (\texttt{nl}), a bare claim (\texttt{claim}), a first-person
belief (\texttt{belief}), the claim appended to the system message
(\texttt{system}), and a two-turn dialogue in which the user states the belief,
the assistant answers ``Okay.'', and the image and question follow
(\texttt{dialogue}; its no-hint cell uses a neutral first turn). Every form
keeps the standard question and the \unknown\ option. The \texttt{std} form
reproduces the main battery cell for cell (3B refusal
$0.904/0.521/0.563$, adoption $0.467/0.433$, wrong-hint accuracy $0.383$; 7B
$0.958/0.877/0.958$, $0.123/0.042$, $0.249$; Gemma $0.843/0.402/0.475$,
$0.579/0.502$, $0.241$), so the battery differs from the main run only in the
form of the assertion. The six-form battery also adds the agreeing-hint cell.

\paragraph{Behaviour depends on the form.}
Table~\ref{tab:sixform-behaviour} reports adoption and wrong-hint accuracy. In
3B adoption of the plausible absent hint spans a factor of $20$, from $0.042$
in \texttt{nl} to $0.885$ in \texttt{system}; the bracketed form ($0.433$) is
statistically indistinguishable from the two-turn dialogue ($0.483$; paired
difference $-0.050$ $[-0.123,+0.023]$) and far below \texttt{system}
($+0.452$ $[+0.391,+0.513]$). For this model the bracketed form is neither
necessary for adoption (system, dialogue) nor the strongest form. Adoption is
highest when the assertion precedes the image (system, dialogue), intermediate
when it is flagged as a hint, and lowest when it is a bare single-turn
sentence after the image. In 7B adoption is at most $0.050$ in every form, so
this model's readout does not take the assertion as the answer whatever its
position.

\paragraph{Geometry survives every form.}
Table~\ref{tab:sixform-geometry} reports the evidence-axis geometry. The axis
shortens to $0.52$--$0.68$ of its no-hint length and turns
$28^\circ$--$47^\circ$ in 3B, and to $0.38$--$0.75$ and $18^\circ$--$47^\circ$
in 7B, in every form. The signature is present in every form; its magnitude
varies. A probe fitted only on \texttt{std} conflict states
transfers to every other form's conflict states at AUROC $0.953$--$0.999$. The
bracketed form is neither necessary for the geometry (five forms without a
bracket reproduce it, including a two-turn dialogue) nor uniquely diluting
(it is the most diluted form in 7B and in Gemma). One caveat: in the five non-dialogue forms the two
no-hint cells are the same prompt, since the assertion is absent, so their
states are identical by construction and the clean axis is one vector shared
across those forms. The conflict axes use form-specific prompts, so the length
ratios, rotations and transfers are not trivial. The dialogue no-hint cell is
an independent test because its neutral first turn changes the prompt, and it
recovers the clean axis at $\cos 0.976$ (3B) and $0.960$ (7B) against the
other forms.

\paragraph{Cross-family replication: the behaviour reverses, the geometry does not.}
Gemma-3-4B-it runs the same six forms. Its behavioural ordering is the mirror
image of Qwen 3B: adoption of the plausible absent hint is $0.502$ in
\texttt{std} and $0.004$--$0.119$ in the other five forms, and the two forms
that are strongest in Qwen, \texttt{system} and \texttt{dialogue}, are the two
weakest in Gemma ($0.034$ and $0.004$); in \texttt{dialogue} the assertion
raises absent refusal above its own no-hint level ($0.743\to0.935/0.962$).
The geometry does not reverse. In every non-\texttt{std} form the axis
shortens to $0.68$--$0.89$ and turns $16^\circ$--$32^\circ$, the two hinted
colours remain nearly parallel ($0.971$--$0.999$), and a \texttt{std}-trained
probe transfers at AUROC $0.969$--$0.997$; \texttt{std} remains the most
diluted form ($0.529$, $28.2^\circ$), matching the earlier prompt-variant
run. A family$\times$placement interaction thus exists in behaviour but not
in the evidence axis: placement changes whether the readout acts on the
assertion, not what the assertion does to the state. One caveat: Gemma's
dialogue no-hint axis is $46^\circ$ from the \texttt{std} no-hint axis
($\cos 0.689$), unlike Qwen ($\cos 0.976$/$0.960$), so its dialogue geometry
is read within-form only.

\paragraph{Reliability, and what the rotation is not.}
Split-half over scenes gives a stable conflict axis ($\cos 0.96$--$0.99$) and
nearly parallel axes for the two hinted colours ($0.91$--$0.99$). The rotation
away from the no-hint axis, measured on one half of the scenes, is only weakly
aligned with the isolated colour-swap direction measured on the other half
($\cos 0.16$--$0.26$ where it is measurable, and indistinguishable from zero in
3B \texttt{nl}, \texttt{claim} and \texttt{belief}), so the assertion does not
add a fixed colour vector to the evidence axis; it reconfigures the axis. That
reconfiguration is not triggered by conflict alone: an added sentence with no
object and no colour turns the axis almost as far, while the compression that
the conflict manipulation amplifies does grow with how much the sentence is
about the queried category (query-specificity control below).

\paragraph{An agreeing hint.}
The new battery also runs the sufficient arm with a hint that agrees with the
image. In Qwen, in 9 of the 12 model-by-form cells the loss under a conflicting hint
exceeds the gain from an agreeing one (3B gains $+0.069$--$+0.211$ against
losses $-0.103$--$-0.379$; 7B gains $-0.050$--$+0.280$ against losses
$-0.115$--$-0.418$); in 3B \texttt{belief}, \texttt{system} and \texttt{dialogue}
the two are within $0.03$ of each other, and in 7B \texttt{nl} and
\texttt{dialogue} an agreeing hint does not help at all. The geometry separates
the cells: with an agreeing hint the evidence axis remains longer than with a
conflicting one in 4/6 forms in 3B and 6/6 in 7B, most sharply in 7B
\texttt{std} ($0.74$ vs.\ $0.38$) and \texttt{claim} ($0.70$ vs.\ $0.39$).
Rotation is present under both and is larger under conflict in 7B. In Qwen,
then, dilution is graded by whether the assertion contradicts the image,
while rotation is what any assertion does. Gemma breaks the grading: there
the agreeing hint dilutes as much as the conflicting one (length $0.54$ vs.\
$0.53$ in \texttt{std}; all six forms within $0.04$) and rotates as much or
more (\texttt{system} $40^\circ$ vs.\ $32^\circ$, \texttt{claim}
$34^\circ$ vs.\ $26^\circ$). The cross-family invariant is that an assertion
moves the axis; whether contradiction adds movement beyond agreement is
model-specific.

\paragraph{The stated colour is linearly decodable in every form.}
An eleven-way decoder trained within the absent-$b$ cell reaches
$0.97$--$0.996$ in 3B and $0.94$--$0.996$ in 7B. Trained on the absent-$a$
cell and tested on the absent-$b$ cell of the same form, where the same scene
carries a different label, it reaches $0.85$--$0.97$ raw ($0.76$--$0.96$
balanced) in 3B, and $0.85$--$0.99$ raw in 7B except the two-turn dialogue
($0.57$ raw, $0.45$ balanced against a $0.38$ majority baseline; the colour is
still decodable within the dialogue's own $b$ cell at $0.94$). A
\texttt{std}-trained decoder transfers to every form's $b$ cell at
$0.86$--$0.96$ in 3B and $0.61$--$0.99$ in 7B, so in Qwen the assertion is
decodable even in forms the model does not adopt. In Gemma within-cell
decoding is equally high ($0.71$--$0.99$), but a within-cell decoder can lean
on category-level colour priors, because the hinted colour is a fixed
function of the absent category; the cross-cell test is the one that excludes
them. It succeeds only in the two forms the model acts on (\texttt{std}
$0.85$, \texttt{claim} $0.66$) and falls to $0.46$, $0.40$, $0.18$ and
$0.10$ in \texttt{system}, \texttt{nl}, \texttt{belief} and
\texttt{dialogue}. In Gemma, therefore, a shared colour code is demonstrable
only where the model acts on the assertion, although the axis moves in every
form.

\paragraph{Attention.}
In 3B (final query, eager attention, 36 layers, 55 scenes) the assertion
receives $3$--$7\%$ of final-query attention in every form, the image
$7$--$10\%$ and the question $17$--$20\%$. The form with the highest adoption
(\texttt{system}, $0.96$) has the lowest assertion share ($0.052$), while
\texttt{nl} (adoption $0.17$) has a higher one ($0.068$), so the between-form
variation in adoption is not a matter of how much the readout attends to the
assertion. Image attention rises from the absent to the sufficient arm in
every form ($0.071\to0.089$ in \texttt{std}, $0.072\to0.104$ in
\texttt{system}). Within a form, scenes that adopt have modestly more
assertion attention ($r=+0.36$ in \texttt{std}, $+0.43$ in \texttt{dialogue},
$+0.17$ in \texttt{system}, absent cell), consistent with the assertion
competing for the readout, but this does not explain the large between-form
differences. This run resizes images to $512$ px for speed, so its own argmax
rates are lower than the battery's; the correlations use the battery's
adoption.

\paragraph{The form-level link is to conflict accuracy.}
Across the six forms the evidence-axis length ratio predicts wrong-hint
accuracy at $r=+0.89$ in both Qwen sizes (Spearman $+0.83$ and $+0.93$) and
at $r=+0.98$ in Gemma (Spearman $+0.94$), while its relation to adoption has
no consistent sign (3B $r=+0.25$, 7B $r=-0.79$ with adoption never above
$0.05$, Gemma $r=-0.83$). The less a form dilutes the axis, the more
accurately the model answers under conflict, in every model tested; whether
the model answers at all is a separate, family-dependent property of the
readout.

\paragraph{What moves the axis: a query-specificity control.}
Every form asserts something about the queried category, so the forms alone
cannot separate query content from the extra sentence or from a colour word
about something else. We add three control forms in the \texttt{nl} template,
each in both evidence arms with no hint: a neutral sentence (``A colleague
mentioned that the room is quiet.''), an assertion about a different object
(``A colleague mentioned that the remote is \emph{colour}.'', where
\texttt{remote} is never a queried or absent category), and an assertion about
the queried category that holds one colour fixed across arms (``A colleague
mentioned that the \emph{category} is \emph{colour}.'', where the colour is
the scene's absent-category colour, so the asserted colour is identical across
arms and is wrong for a present scene). The evidence axis is
sufficient-minus-absent within each form, so the only change from $v_0$ is the
added sentence (Table~\ref{tab:query-control}). Compression is graded by how
much the sentence is about the query. In 3B the neutral sentence leaves the axis at $0.822$
$[0.803,0.840]$ of the no-hint length, the other-object assertion at $0.804$
$[0.787,0.822]$, the constant-colour query assertion at $0.604$
$[0.587,0.622]$, and the \texttt{nl} conflict axis at $0.589$; the same order
holds in 7B ($0.730$, $0.691$, $0.596$, $0.568$); Gemma is compressed
throughout ($1.000$, $0.897$, $0.877$, $0.885$), with its query and conflict
rows within $0.01$ of each other. The direction is query-linked as well: the
constant-colour query axis is nearly collinear with the conflict axis ($\cos
0.976$ in 3B, $0.988$ in 7B, $0.997$ in Gemma), whereas the neutral and
other-object axes are further away (cosine with the conflict axis
$0.795$/$0.865$, $0.874$/$0.921$ and $0.964$/$0.978$). The paired contrast
isolates the object the sentence is about: naming the queried category
instead of another object turns the axis a further $7.4^\circ$ $[5.8,9.0]$
in 3B and $2.8^\circ$ $[1.5,4.0]$ in 7B, but only $1.0^\circ$ $[0.5,1.6]$
in Gemma, and shortens it by $0.20$, $0.10$ and $0.02$ (Gemma's difference
touches zero, $[-0.05,0.01]$). Behaviour follows the compression in 3B: with
evidence, accuracy is $0.762$ with no hint, $0.736$ with the neutral
sentence, $0.705$ with the other-object assertion, $0.571$ with the
constant-colour query sentence and $0.567$ under \texttt{nl} conflict; the
query sentence raises reporting of its (absent-category) colour from $12.3\%$
to $21.8\%$, lowers absent-arm refusal from $0.973$--$0.977$ to $0.866$
($0.904$ with no hint) and raises refusal under evidence from
$0.084$--$0.092$ to $0.192$. In 7B the query sentence raises adoption from
$7.7\%$ to $14.9\%$; in Gemma accuracy is flat across the first three cells
($0.567$, $0.552$, $0.563$), and only the query sentence lowers it to $0.521$
and raises adoption to $19.9\%$. We therefore read the conflict effect as the
extreme of a graded, query-relevance-dependent compression: adding text moves
the axis, but the component that the conflict manipulation amplifies---a
shortening plus a turn toward the conflict direction---grows with how much the
sentence is about the queried category, and appears even when the asserted
colour does not change between arms. The residual difference between the
constant-colour query axis and the conflict axis is the colour change itself,
which adds a small further shortening in 3B and 7B ($0.604\to0.589$ and
$0.596\to0.568$) and nothing in Gemma ($0.877\to0.885$).

\section{Second stimulus set: PASCAL VOC 2012}
\label{app:voc}

The main set is built on COCO. To check that the construction rather than a COCO peculiarity produces the pattern, we rebuilt the six cells on PASCAL VOC 2012 segmentation \citep{everingham2010pascal}: $180$ images from the val split (long side at most $1024$ px), each contributing one present item and one annotation-absent item, with present instances passing the same component, box-size, fill and colour-purity gates as the main set and absent categories being VOC classes with no annotated pixel in the image, hinted with that class's most frequent colour in the split. Labels come from the automatic gates only, with no contact-sheet verification, and the same Qwen2.5-VL-3B checkpoint, prompts and readout are used.

Behaviour again moves both arms in opposite directions. Without a hint the model refuses $0.894$ of absent items and names the present item's colour correctly on $0.422$; the plausible hint lowers absent refusal to $0.644$ and is adopted on $0.339$ (far hue: $0.683$ and $0.311$), while on present items the wrong hint raises refusal from $0.011$ to $0.400$ and lowers gold accuracy from $0.422$ to $0.217$. The correct hint raises accuracy to $0.839$.

The probe transfers. Fitted on the no-hint pair with scene-grouped folds it reaches AUROC $0.994$ [$0.987$, $0.999$], and refitted inside the conflict pair $0.998$ [$0.996$, $1.000$]. Fitted on training scenes' no-hint pairs and applied unchanged to held-out scenes it reaches $0.983$ [$0.971$, $0.992$] under the correct hint, $0.925$ [$0.895$, $0.955$] under the plausible hint and $0.950$ [$0.921$, $0.975$] under the far-hue hint.

The geometry replicates. The present--absent mean difference shortens to $0.61$, $0.41$ and $0.52$ of its no-hint length and turns $28^\circ$ [$26^\circ$, $31^\circ$], $35^\circ$ [$33^\circ$, $38^\circ$] and $33^\circ$ [$31^\circ$, $37^\circ$] from the no-hint axis; per-scene coherence falls from $0.61$ to $0.43$, $0.31$ and $0.39$, and the two hinted colours coincide at $\cos=0.89$ [$0.84$, $0.92$]. The effect is not a COCO artefact.

Patching localises the gate on this stimulus set as well. Pasting the image's own no-hint absent state back into the conflict pass recovers $-24\%$ at layer 16 ($44\%$ depth), $29\%$ at layer 24 ($67\%$), $84\%$ at layer 30 ($83\%$) and $100\%$ at layer 34 ($94\%$); a shuffled absent source gives $-28\%$, $24\%$, $98\%$ and $102\%$. Recovery is again late, steep and category-level rather than sample-specific.

The law's form transfers but its gain does not, and the difference is diagnostic. The within-class spread contracts to $0.88$, $0.82$ and $0.79$ of its no-hint value, against $0.71$ on the main set. Because the $d'$ ratio is the gap-to-spread ratio, the through-origin slope fitted here is $1.20$, and the main-set slope $1.425$ over-predicts the three cells by $0.088$--$0.175$ (RMSE $0.123$). Per cell the inverse spread contractions are $1.14$, $1.21$ and $1.27$, so the fitted slope again tracks $1/\text{spread contraction}$, as it does on the main set ($1/0.71=1.41$ against a fitted $1.42$). The linear relation is preserved; the constant multiplying it is a property of how much noise the hint removes in a given stimulus set and model, not a universal gain.

Signal detection and the gate follow the main set. Sensitivity falls from $d'=3.44$ to $0.67$ under conflict ($\Delta$ [$ -3.52$, $-2.35$]) while the criterion moves from $+0.48$ to $-0.09$ ($\Delta$ [$ -0.89$, $-0.34$]); the correct hint leaves $d'=2.61$. At $25\%$ answered coverage the frozen probe's fabrication rate is $0.144$ [$0.078$, $0.211$], against $0.367$ [$0.272$, $0.444$] for $\punk$ and $0.422$ [$0.356$, $0.517$] for maximum probability (paired difference $[-0.300$, $-0.133]$); at $50\%$ coverage, where the model answers $0.478$ of conflict items, all three tie at $0.372$ (paired difference $[-0.020$, $0]$). As on the main set, the gate helps at low coverage and not at high.

\section{Second backbone outside the Qwen family: Phi-3.5-vision}
\label{app:phi}

Gemma-3-4B was our first non-Qwen point, and its output signal does not collapse. To test whether the collapse is tied to the Qwen2/2.5 readouts or to one training recipe, we ran the full battery on Phi-3.5-vision-instruct (a Phi-3-mini language model with a CLIP ViT-L/14-336 encoder), loading its native remote code with eager attention because its config pins flash-attention-2, and using its native prompt format. All six cells and the final-token state ($3072$-d) were collected for the same $261$ scenes; labels, prompts, readout and analysis are unchanged. It qualifies as an abstaining backbone: it refuses $0.897$ of absent items without a hint.

Conflict moves both arms in opposite directions and, in this model, past each other. Absent refusal falls from $0.897$ to $0.322$ (hint \texttt{a}) and $0.425$ (hint \texttt{b}), with adoption $0.663$ and $0.567$; on present items the wrong hint raises refusal from $0.119$ to $0.548$ and lowers gold accuracy from $0.625$ to $0.080$, while the correct hint raises it to $0.858$. Present refusal therefore exceeds absent refusal under conflict, the reverse of the no-hint ordering. The own-signal detector follows: $\punk$ AUROC falls from $0.939$ [$0.918$, $0.958$] to $0.385$ [$0.336$, $0.433$] and $0.458$ [$0.408$, $0.506$], below chance in both hinted cells, and maximum probability is near chance throughout ($0.442$/$0.492$/$0.492$). On the same items the internal probe reaches $0.992$ [$0.987$, $0.997$] with no hint and $0.998$ [$0.996$, $1.000$] refitted inside the conflict pair.

The geometry is the extreme case of the pattern. The present--absent mean difference shortens to $0.525$ [$0.470$, $0.575$] under the correct hint and to $0.052$ [$0.006$, $0.113$] and $0.149$ [$0.096$, $0.212$] under the plausible and far-hue hints, and turns $52.8^\circ$ [$49.4^\circ$, $56.8^\circ$], $85.9^\circ$ [$80.9^\circ$, $89.5^\circ$] and $74.4^\circ$ [$67.4^\circ$, $80.2^\circ$] from the no-hint axis. Per-scene coherence falls from $0.388$ to $0.234$, $0.027$ and $0.070$, and the two hinted colours still point the same way ($\cos=0.720$ [$0.646$, $0.790$]). The near-orthogonal rotation is why the frozen probe falls to $0.954$ [$0.936$, $0.971$] under the correct hint and to $0.685$ [$0.640$, $0.731$] / $0.675$ [$0.633$, $0.718$] under the two conflicting hints, the weakest transfer here, even though the same states are decodable at $0.998$ when the readout is refitted. This is the sharpest form of the dissociation: the evidence is present and linearly decodable, but the no-hint direction has almost no projection onto it. The linear law keeps its form but not its gain: applying the four-model slope $1.425$ out of sample over-predicts Phi's three cells by $0.024$, $0.075$ and $0.145$ (RMSE $0.096$), and Phi's own through-origin slope is $1.13$, below every fitted model's gain, so the constant remains model-specific as reported above.

Signal detection is a full reversal rather than a criterion shift: $d'$ goes from $2.43$ [$2.18$, $2.71$] to $-0.44$ [$ -0.63$, $-0.26$] ($\Delta$ [$ -3.19$, $-2.58$]) and the criterion from $-0.04$ [$ -0.17$, $0.10$] to $+0.10$ [$+0.01$, $+0.21$]; the correct hint leaves $d'=2.57$. The gate still works where the own signal has reversed: at $25\%$ answered coverage the frozen probe's fabrication rate is $0.285$ [$0.208$, $0.377$], against $0.562$ [$0.477$, $0.631$] for $\punk$ and $0.662$ [$0.577$, $0.723$] for maximum probability (paired difference $[-0.354$, $-0.192]$), and at $50\%$ coverage it is $0.556$ [$0.498$, $0.609$] against $0.586$ [$0.544$, $0.632$] and $0.636$ [$0.594$, $0.678$] (paired difference $[-0.073$, $-0.004]$). The advantage is large at low coverage and marginal at high, as in the main models. The category-matched control and the conformal scan, run after an earlier draft, do not change this. Within a fixed question the frozen probe reaches $0.592$ [$0.530$, $0.657$] against $0.446$ for $\punk$ and $0.950$ for the refit, and the gate's fabrication rate at $25\%$ answered coverage is $0.438$ against $0.446$ (probe) and $0.592$ (maximum probability): the ranking margin over a below-chance own signal does not translate into a meaningful operating-point advantage. Conformal certification is infeasible at both $\alpha=5\%$ and $10\%$ in all $20$ splits (minimum UCB $0.305$). The prompt-format controls reproduce the scope conditions: removing \unknown\ raises adoption from $0.663$ to $0.985$, sentence phrasing raises conflict refusal from $0.322$ to $0.877$ and cuts adoption to $0.088$, and wrong-hint accuracy rises from $0.080$ to $0.276$ (Table~\ref{tab:promptfmt}). Patching localises the gate as in the other backbones: the image's own no-hint absent state recovers $31\%$ at layer 14 ($44\%$ depth), $97\%$ at layer 22 ($69\%$), $102\%$ at layer 28 ($88\%$) and $101\%$ at layer 30 ($94\%$); the present-question state drives refusal below base at the deep layers ($-0.27$ and $-0.33$), and shuffled absent sources recover as much as the own state ($1.07$/$1.05$). Its role is scope: the geometry replicates in a third language-model family and a third vision encoder, the output collapse is not specific to Qwen, and the gate is written late, while Gemma remains the counterexample that separates the representation from the readout.

although it removes $5.9\times$ the energy of a random projection. In the
layers, L16 moves the gate in both directions but also collapses present-item
gold accuracy and hint adoption (present move $0.674$), L24's negative
direction is similarly destructive ($0.375$), and L8 lowers refusal at both
signs. L30 (88\% depth) is the only clean site: absent refusal $-0.151$

Phi's direction is privileged. At the head it is $3.0\times$ the mean random
effect on absent refusal ($0.220$ against $0.073$) and $12.9\times$ for
ablation, and it moves present refusal and present gold coherently with absent

patching recovers only $31\%$ at L14 and $97\%$ at L22. The axis is therefore
available to the readout before the conflict-induced loss is localised:
injection establishes sufficiency of the direction, not the site where the
conflict acts. Neither model has a random-direction control at layer sites, so
the layer profiles are descriptive rather than direction-specific.

\section{Per-layer probe}
\label{app:layer}

A probe refitted independently at each layer (grouped by scene) reaches AUROC $0.500$ at the embedding output, $0.867$ at layer 4, $0.982$ at layer 8, and $\ge0.990$ from layer 16 onward; transfer from the no-hint pair to the conflicting pair has a dip at layer 8 ($0.803$) and recovers to $0.965$--$0.995$ deeper. Refitting, however, gives each layer its own estimator, so this curve measures decodability rather than a shared axis. We therefore also fit the estimator once on the final layer (per fold) and apply its weights unchanged to every layer, with per-layer standardisation only. That shared readout is at chance through position 18 of the stored stack (embedding plus 36 layers; AUROC $\le0.53$, rising to $0.58$ and $0.64$ at positions 19 and 21), then jumps to $0.86$ at position 22, $0.97$ at position 24 and $0.99$ from position 25 onward. The rise is not monotone ($0.67$ at position 23), so we locate it to the last quarter rather than a single layer. Evidence is thus linearly decodable early, but in a layer-specific basis; the final-layer evidence axis used by steering and patching is assembled late. The two analyses share a target but not an estimator, and we do not map probe AUROC at layer $L$ one-to-one onto recovery at layer $L$.

\section{Construct and predictive validity}
\label{app:validity}

Two checks separate what the probe measures from what it does not. Construct validity asks whether the present--absent separation is an artefact of answer correctness: restricting the present arm to items the model answered \emph{wrong} leaves the separation essentially unchanged, and within-present correct-vs-wrong is far weaker. The probe tracks evidence presence, not answerability.

Predictive validity asks whether the score predicts individual conflict behaviours. It predicts absent-item refusal in 3B, 7B and Gemma; in Gemma it also predicts whether a correct present answer survives and whether the hint is adopted. It does not predict adoption in 3B/7B, predicts nothing in LLaVA, where refusal is near zero and adoption near ceiling, and in Phi-3.5 the conflict scores point the wrong way (AUROC $0.392$ for absent refusal, $0.376$ for present gold and $0.440$ for adoption), consistent with its weak frozen transfer. Construct validity holds in Phi: the no-hint probe separates present from absent at $0.992$ even on present items the model answers wrongly ($0.987$) and is far weaker within the present arm ($0.680$). The gate therefore works on average without being a calibrated per-item predictor of credulity: the score measures evidence availability, not the strength of the hint override.

\section{Deployment details}

Scores are scene-cross-fitted for all five models: the probe is fitted on the no-hint pairs of four scene folds and applied to the conflict pairs of the held-out fold. All deployment numbers use the plausible hint \texttt{a} and pair \texttt{sufficient|wrong} with \texttt{absent|a}, giving $522$ items. Fabrication is the fraction of answered items that are absent-object items; coverage is the fraction of all items the system answers, and each scorer's threshold is chosen to reach the stated answered coverage. The 3B refit gate reaches $0.100$ at $50\%$ answered coverage; the cross-fitted score reaches $0.115$. The 12-dimensional logit readout reaches $0.142$ at $50\%$ selection coverage. Conformal calibration uses half the scenes, a Clopper--Pearson upper bound at $90\%$ confidence over a 50-point threshold scan, family-wise correction over the scan, and evaluation on the other half; the answered subset is used in both numerator and denominator.

\section{Compute}
\label{app:compute}

All runs used one NVIDIA A10 with 24 GB. The main 3B run (six conditions per image, hidden states saved) takes about 30 minutes; the 7B and LLaVA runs take about an hour each; the Gemma-3-4B run takes about six minutes at a single $512$-pixel resize; the full suite, including steering, patching and external validation, fits in a few GPU-hours.
